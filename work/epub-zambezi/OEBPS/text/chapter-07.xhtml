🗝️ CHAPTER SEVEN

The Secret Cavern of Batoka Gorge

The Lost Rock Paintings of the Ancient Hunters

📍 Batoka Gorge & Livingstone Escarpment

Two brave children carrying a brass lantern marvel at ancient red-ochre rock art inside an emerald underground pool cavern.

Far downstream from Victoria Falls, the Zambezi River cuts through the zigzagging canyons of the Batoka Gorge. Here, sheer basalt walls rise hundreds of meters above turbulent Class V rapids. In these shadow-cloaked cliffs lie hidden caverns known only to cliff-nesting falcons and wandering goat herders.

During school holidays, two adventurous cousins, Mapalo and Kunda, were helping their uncle search for a stray calf along the high rim of the third gorge. Following faint hoofprints down a steep goat track, Mapalo slipped slightly, catching himself against a cleft in the rock face. Behind a curtain of wild jasmine vines, they noticed a cool breeze drifting from an arched opening in the stone.

Mapalo lit an old brass kerosene lantern he carried in his satchel. Holding the yellow flame aloft, the cousins stepped cautiously into the cavern. The roaring sound of the river outside immediately faded into a reverent, cavernous hush.

Before them opened a breathtaking subterranean chamber. At their feet lay a pool of crystalline turquoise water so calm it resembled polished glass. But what made both children gasp was the cavern wall: stretching across the limestone was a magnificent gallery of ancient rock art painted in rich red ochre, charcoal, and white clay.

There were leaping elands with curved horns, running hunters carrying light bows, swimming hippos, and at the center, a gigantic arched river serpent crowned with fish fins. 'Look,' whispered Kunda with wide, glistening eyes. 'The ancestors were here thousands of years ago. They painted our river spirit before paper or books existed!'

Mapalo and Kunda sat by the lantern light, tracing the centuries-old outlines with their eyes without touching the fragile pigment. When they emerged into the afternoon sunshine, they did not reveal the cavern to treasure hunters; instead, they reported their discovery to the Livingstone Museum curators, ensuring the ancient sanctuary would be preserved with the dignity and protection it deserved.

Ancestral Moral Lesson

“True heritage is our shared responsibility to discover, honor, and protect for those who come after us.”

📖 Cultural Vocabulary

Batoka Gorge: The dramatic, narrow 120-kilometer canyon carved by the Zambezi River downstream from Victoria Falls.
Red Ochre: A natural clay earth pigment containing ferric oxide, used for millennia by indigenous African artists for rock paintings.
Subterranean: Existing, occurring, or situated beneath the surface of the earth.
Sanctuary: A place of safety, refuge, or sacred preservation.

2. What images were painted on the cavern walls?

  4. Ocean sailing ships

Answer (b): The walls featured ancient ochre paintings of elands, hunters, hippos, and the sacred river serpent.

3. What responsible action did the children take after leaving the cave?

  1. They sold souvenirs to tourists
  2. They informed the Livingstone Museum curators to safeguard the site
  3. They carved their own names on the cave paintings
  4. They sealed the cave entrance with rocks

Answer (b): The children protected heritage by reporting the discovery to museum curators for preservation.
