🔥 CHAPTER SIX

The Mukuni Fire Walkers and the Sacred Flame

The Legend of the Timeless Campfire at Batoka

📍 Mukuni Village & Batoka Gorge (Toka-Leya Traditions)

Wise elder Mukuni holding his carved serpent staff beside the sacred fire, recounting oral histories to attentive youth under a starry sky.

Perched atop the ancient plateau overlooking the roaring gorge of Livingstone lies Mukuni Village, one of the oldest continuously inhabited royal settlements in southern Africa. In the central *insaka*—the village gathering circle—a small log fire has been kept burning for hundreds of years. The Toka-Leya people call it *Mulilo wa Kale*, the Fire of Remembrance.

According to oral tradition, this sacred fire was originally kindled from a lightning strike that touched a towering mopane tree during the great drought of antiquity. Ever since, each generation of elders has tended the coals with aromatic wild olive wood, ensuring the village never loses its warmth, its counsel, or its memory.

Each new moon, the youth of Mukuni gather in a wide semi-circle around the coals. On this evening, an elder named Bwana Sikute leaned upon his carved serpent staff. His silver hair caught the flickering amber light as he spoke to the attentive children: 'A tribe without its stories is like a tree whose roots have forgotten the soil. It may look green for a season, but the first storm will topple it.'

A young boy named Chanda asked, 'Grandfather, why do we not simply use matches to light a new fire whenever we need one? Why carry the coals through rain and wind?'

Sikute smiled warmly and placed a dry twig onto the glowing heart of the fire. Within seconds, a joyful golden flame leaped upward, showering the night with fragrant cedar sparks. 'Because, Chanda, this fire did not begin with us, and it will not end with us. Every spark you see contains the laughter of your grandmothers, the courage of your grandfathers, and the prayers of ancestors who walked these cliffs before kings had names.'

Chanda bowed his head, feeling the warmth spread across his hands and heart. He understood then that the fire was not just burning wood; it was the sacred, unbroken chain of culture, belonging, and community that bonded all their people into one.

Ancestral Moral Lesson

“Knowledge passed from elders to the young is a living flame; when nurtured with respect, it never extinguishes.”

📖 Cultural Vocabulary

Insaka: A traditional Zambian open-air village pavilion or round shelter where community meetings, meals, and storytelling take place.
Toka-Leya: The indigenous people inhabiting the southern Zambezi borderlands around Victoria Falls and Livingstone.
Mopane: A resilient hardwood tree prevalent in southern African river valleys, prized for durable wood and fragrant aroma.
Mulilo wa Kale: Tonga/Leya phrase meaning 'The Ancient Fire' or 'The Eternal Fire of Ancestral Memory.'

❓ Wonder & Comprehension Questions

1. What is the name of the traditional village storytelling circle?

  1. Boma
  2. Insaka
  3. Chitenge
  4. Musika

Answer (b): An 'insaka' is the traditional open-air pavilion and community circle used for elder storytelling.

2. What did the elder Sikute say a tribe without its stories resembles?

  1. A river without fish
  2. A tree whose roots have forgotten the soil
  3. A bow without arrows
  4. A night without stars

Answer (b): Sikute taught that without oral heritage, people are like trees with forgotten roots that fall in storms.

3. What does the eternal campfire symbolize to the villagers of Mukuni?

  1. A way to keep wild animals away
  2. An unbroken chain of heritage, ancestral memory, and community love
  3. A signal for neighboring warriors
  4. A method for cooking daily food only

Answer (b): The fire symbolizes the living, unbroken continuum of ancestral wisdom and shared communal identity.
