ORIGINAL STORY THREE

Jelita, Mulenga and the School Garden

The environmental club at Chisomo Primary School received a neglected patch of ground behind the classroom block.

The soil was hard, weeds reached Jelita's knees and part of the wire fence had fallen against an old stump.

The club teacher, Mrs Lungu, gave the pupils a challenge. "Turn this forgotten place into a useful garden before the end of the term."

Jelita imagined baskets of tomatoes, onions and leafy vegetables for the school kitchen.

Mulenga stretched his arms wide. "I will grow the largest pumpkin anyone in this district has ever seen."

On the first Saturday, nearly every club member arrived. They cleared weeds, loosened soil and planted seeds. By midday, neat beds covered the patch.

They celebrated as if the harvest had already arrived.

When Excitement Fades

During the next two weeks, the garden began to struggle.

Some pupils forgot their watering days. Goats pushed through the weak fence and ate young leaves. The sun hardened the uncovered soil, and weeds returned between the rows.

Mulenga blamed the goats. Other pupils blamed the heat. One group accused another of using too much water.

Jelita opened the club notebook. The first page listed many exciting ideas, but no one had written who would complete each task or when it should be done.

"The weather is difficult," she said, ?but our biggest problem is that we started without a plan."

Mrs Lungu did not solve the problem for them. Instead, she asked, "What does a living garden need every week, not only on the first day?"

The pupils listed water, healthy soil, protection from animals, weeding and careful observation.

Many Small Jobs

They formed teams. One repaired the fence with straight poles and stronger wire. Another collected dry grass for mulch. A third checked plants for pests.

Jelita created a picture schedule using symbols for watering cans, hoes, leaves and fences. Even the youngest members could see their duties.

Mulenga collected used plastic bottles, cleaned them and made tiny holes in the lids. He buried each bottle beside a plant so water would drip slowly toward the roots.

The first dripper emptied too quickly. The second barely released any water.

Mulenga adjusted the holes until the flow was steady. "An invention sometimes needs patience before it becomes useful," he said.

Jelita wanted straight planting rows because they were easy to measure and weed. Mulenga wanted curved beds that followed the slope and caught rainwater.

Their disagreement grew louder until Mrs Lungu asked a simple question: "How can you discover which idea works without arguing?"

They planted one small straight section and one curved section. After the next rain, the curved bed held more water, while the straight rows were easier to weed.

Instead of choosing one winner, the club combined the ideas. They used curved borders to slow water and straight inner rows for crops.

The Night of the Goats

One evening, a gatepost came loose and three goats entered the garden.

A pupil passing nearby raised the alarm. Jelita, Mulenga and two neighbours guided the goats out without frightening them.

The pumpkin vine had been trampled, but its main stem was still alive.

Mulenga wanted to pull it out and start again. Jelita showed him a new green shoot beneath the damaged leaves.

They supported the vine with sticks, added compost around its roots and gave it time to recover.

Within weeks, yellow flowers opened along the stem.

A Harvest for Everyone

By the end of term, the neglected patch had become a green outdoor classroom.

The club harvested tomatoes, rape, onions and beans. Mulenga's pumpkin was so heavy that two pupils carried it on a wooden board.

They sold part of the harvest and used the money to buy seeds and repair tools. They gave another part to the school kitchen and saved the healthiest seeds for the next season.

At the harvest celebration, Jelita pointed to the duty schedule, now marked with many completed tasks.

"The garden did not grow because of one brilliant idea," she said. "It grew because many people completed small responsibilities."

Mulenga placed a hand on the giant pumpkin. "It also grew because we tested our different ideas instead of trying to defeat one another."

The garden became a place where pupils studied soil, insects, nutrition, measurement and teamwork.

Long after the celebration ended, the bottle drippers continued their quiet work'one careful drop at a time.

Lesson from the Story

Teamwork succeeds when people organise, cooperate and complete their responsibilities.

New Words

  • Neglected: not given proper care
  • Mulch: material placed over soil to protect it
  • Schedule: a plan showing when tasks should happen
  • Harvest: crops gathered when they are ready

Knowledge Check / Think About It

1. Why did the garden struggle after the first Saturday?

A. The club lacked an organised plan.

B. No seeds were planted.

C. Gardens cannot grow at school.

Answer: A

2. How did the club organise the work?

A. One pupil did everything.

B. They formed teams and made a schedule.

C. They abandoned it.

Answer: B

3. What did Jelita and Mulenga do when their ideas differed?

A. They stopped speaking.

B. They destroyed both beds.

C. They tested both ideas and combined what worked.

Answer: C

4. Which action best demonstrates teamwork?

A. Completing an agreed watering duty.

B. Waiting for others.

C. Taking food without helping.

Answer: A
