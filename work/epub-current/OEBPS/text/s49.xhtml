LANSDOWN COLLECTION 9

How Birds Learned to Speak

Long ago birds and bats were friends. In those days neither birds nor bats could fly, and neither could speak as they do today. Both groups suffered greatly because human beings kept them as servants. At last they met and said, "Why should we continue suffering merely because we cannot fly?

Let us ask our Creator for help." Their Creator listened and said, "I will consider your request." When they returned the next evening, he said, "I shall give you wings." He gave wings to both the birds and the bats. The birds were delighted and sang songs of thanks to their Creator.

The bats, however, forgot to thank him. For their ingratitude, the Creator separated them from the birds and allowed them to fly and speak only in darkness. That is why bats fly and call only at night, while birds sing by day in gratitude to the one who freed them.

Lesson from the Story

Gratitude matters when help has been given.

New Words

  • Creator: the one who made them
  • Delighted: very pleased
  • Ingratitude: failing to be thankful

Knowledge Check / Think About It

1. What problem begins How Birds Learned to Speak?

A. Birds refused to fly.

B. Birds hid from daylight.

C. Birds thanked the Creator for wings.

Answer: C

2. What choice changes How Birds Learned to Speak?

A. Bats became fish.

B. Bats forgot to give thanks.

C. Bats gave away their wings.

Answer: B

3. What lesson can readers learn from How Birds Learned to Speak?

A. Gratitude matters after receiving help.

B. Night is better than day.

C. Wings should never be used.

Answer: A

Source
