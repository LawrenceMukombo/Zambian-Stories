🌽 CHAPTER ELEVEN

The Lazy Brother and the Hardworking Sister

A Story About Effort and Preparation

In a quiet village surrounded by beautiful hills lived a brother and sister.

The sister’s name was Ngambo.

The brother’s name was Mwansa.

They lived with their grandmother in a small but happy home.

Their house was not big.

It was not filled with expensive things.

But it was full of something more valuable.

Love.

Every evening, after eating supper, Grandmother sat outside with the children.

She told them stories.

She taught them lessons.

She reminded them about life.

One lesson she repeated many times was:

“Children, the field does not remember the person who talked. The field remembers the person who worked.”

Ngambo understood.

Mwansa did not.

Two Different Children

Although Ngambo and Mwansa grew up in the same home, they were very different.

Ngambo woke up early.

When the first rooster crowed…

KOKOLOKOOOO!

Ngambo was already awake.

She swept the yard.

She collected water.

She helped Grandmother prepare breakfast.

After school, she finished her homework before playing.

Grandmother was proud of her.

“You understand that today’s work prepares tomorrow’s happiness,” she said.

Ngambo smiled.

Mwansa was different.

He loved sleeping.

When the rooster crowed…

Mwansa turned to the other side.

When Grandmother called:

“Wake up, Mwansa!”

He answered:

“Just five more minutes.”

But five minutes became thirty minutes.

Thirty minutes became one hour.

By the time Mwansa woke up, everyone else was already working.

The Coming Rainy Season

One evening, dark clouds gathered above the village.

The smell of rain filled the air.

Grandmother looked at the sky.

“The rains are coming.”

The children became excited.

In the village, rain meant many things.

It meant rivers would flow again.

It meant grass would grow.

Most importantly…

It meant planting season.

The next morning, Grandmother called Ngambo and Mwansa.

“This year, each of you will have a small field.”

The children looked surprised.

“Our own fields?” asked Ngambo.

“Yes.”

Grandmother smiled.

“You are growing older.”

She gave each child some maize seeds.

“Plant these carefully.”

She looked at both of them.

“At harvest time, your field will show your effort.”

Ngambo’s Field

The next morning, Ngambo woke early.

She carried her hoe.

She walked to her field.

The ground was hard.

The work was difficult.

The sun became hot.

But she continued.

Slowly…

One row became two rows.

Two rows became five rows.

Five rows became many.

Every day after school, she checked her plants.

She removed weeds.

She protected the young maize.

Sometimes her friends called:

“Ngambo! Come and play!”

She answered:

“I will come after finishing my work.”

And she did.

Because Ngambo understood something important.

Work and enjoyment can live together when there is discipline.

Mwansa’s Field

Mwansa also started well.

On the first day, he cleared a small part of his field.

“This is easy,” he said.

The next day, his friends came.

“Come, Mwansa! Let us go swimming.”

Mwansa looked at his field.

Then at his friends.

“I will work tomorrow.”

So he went swimming.

Tomorrow came.

But another excuse came with it.

“The sun is too hot today.”

The next day:

“I am too tired.”

The next day:

“I still have enough time.”

Days passed.

The weeds grew.

The grass became tall.

His maize seeds remained in the bag.

Grandmother’s Warning

One evening, Grandmother walked past Mwansa’s field.

She stopped.

She looked at the weeds.

Then she called him.

“Mwansa.”

“Yes, Grandmother.”

“What happened here?”

Mwansa laughed.

“Do not worry. I will fix it.”

“When?”

“Soon.”

Grandmother became serious.

“My child, time is like a river.”

Mwansa listened.

“It keeps moving.”

“You can walk with it, but you cannot call back the water that has already passed.”

Mwansa nodded.

But he still did not understand.

Harvest Time Arrives

Months passed.

The rains ended.

The maize became ready.

Everywhere villagers were harvesting.

There was laughter.

There was singing.

Families carried bags of maize home.

Ngambo walked to her field.

She smiled.

Her maize plants stood tall.

The cobs were big.

Grandmother helped her harvest.

“You worked hard,” she said.

“And your field remembers.”

Ngambo felt proud.

Then Mwansa went to his field.

He stopped.

There was almost nothing.

Only weeds.

A few weak maize plants stood between the grass.

The cobs were tiny.

Mwansa looked down.

For the first time, he understood.

The field had answered him.

A Difficult Lesson

That evening, Ngambo prepared roasted maize.

The smell filled the yard.

Mwansa sat quietly.

Usually he joked.

Usually he laughed.

But not today.

Grandmother noticed.

“What troubles you?”

Mwansa looked down.

“I failed.”

Grandmother shook her head.

“No.”

Mwansa looked surprised.

“But my field produced nothing.”

“Yes.”

“So I failed.”

Grandmother smiled.

“You only fail when you refuse to learn.”

Ngambo broke her maize into two pieces.

She gave half to Mwansa.

He looked surprised.

“You are sharing?”

“Yes.”

“But I did not work.”

Ngambo smiled.

“You are my brother.”

Mwansa felt ashamed.

“I wasted my time while you worked.”

A New Mwansa

The next planting season came.

Before sunrise, Grandmother heard a sound outside.

Chak! Chak! Chak!

Someone was digging.

She opened the door.

It was Mwansa.

He was already working.

Grandmother smiled.

“Who woke you up?”

Mwansa laughed.

“The lesson from last year.”

From that day, Mwansa changed.

He still played.

He still laughed.

But he learned something:

First responsibility.

Then enjoyment.

At harvest time, both fields were full.

Ngambo’s field.

And Mwansa’s field.

Grandmother looked proudly at them.

“Now you understand.”

“The best harvest does not begin in the field.”

“It begins in your attitude.”

Lesson from the Story

Hard work and preparation bring success. Waiting until tomorrow can make opportunities disappear.

New Words

  • Discipline – controlling yourself to do what is right:
  • Excuse – a reason given to avoid doing something:
  • Opportunity – a chance to do something good:

4. Opportunity – a chance to do something good

Knowledge Check / Think About It

1. Why did Ngambo succeed?

A. She slept while weeds covered the field.

B. She waited for someone else to plant everything.

C. She worked steadily and prepared during the growing season.

Answer: C

2. Why did Mwansa’s field fail?

A. Mwansa delayed the work and allowed weeds to take over.

B. The maize grew too quickly.

C. Ngambo secretly removed every plant.

Answer: A

3. Was Ngambo right to share her maize?

A. No. Hungry family members should never be helped.

B. Yes. She showed compassion while helping him learn responsibility.

C. No. Sharing always rewards laziness.

Answer: B

4. What small things can you work hard at today to prepare for tomorrow?

A. Complete schoolwork or reading

B. Help with a regular household task

C. Practise a useful skill
