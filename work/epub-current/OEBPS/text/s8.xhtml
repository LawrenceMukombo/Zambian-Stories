Journey to Tanzania – Part One

The Journey Begins

The school bell rang loudly.

KENG! KENG! KENG!

The sound echoed across Chilanga Primary School.

Children quickly packed their books.

Some ran outside to play football.

Others rushed home because the school holidays had finally arrived.

Everyone was excited.

No homework.

No early morning rushing.

No uniforms.

Just weeks of freedom.

But among all the children leaving school that afternoon, nobody was more excited than Ngambo.

Ngambo walked home faster than usual.

His shoes kicked up small clouds of dust along the road.

His school bag bounced on his back.

He smiled all the way.

Why?

Because these holidays were not ordinary holidays.

For the first time in his life…

Ngambo was going to travel outside Zambia.

He was going to Tanzania.

A Special Letter

Two weeks earlier, Ngambo’s father had received a letter.

In those days, letters were very special.

There were no mobile phones in every pocket.

There was no instant messaging.

Families waited days or weeks for news.

When the postman arrived, everyone became excited.

“Letter! Letter!” shouted Ngambo’s younger sister.

Their father opened it carefully.

“It is from your Uncle Joseph.”

Everyone became quiet.

Uncle Joseph was Ngambo’s father’s younger brother.

Many years earlier, he had moved to Tanzania for work.

He lived in Dar es Salaam, a big city near the ocean.

Father began reading.

"Dear Brother,

I hope everyone at home is well.

It has been many years since I saw the children. I would like Ngambo and his cousin Mwansa to visit me during the school holidays.

They are growing up, and travelling will teach them many things that books alone cannot teach.

They will see new places, meet new people, and learn about another country.

Your brother, Joseph."

Ngambo could hardly believe it.

“Tanzania?”

“Yes,” said Father.

“The country next to Zambia?”

Father laughed.

“Yes.”

“The one with Mount Kilimanjaro?”

“Yes.”

“The one with the Indian Ocean?”

“Yes.”

Ngambo jumped happily.

“I am going to see the ocean!”

Everyone laughed.

Preparing for the Journey

The days before travelling moved very slowly.

At least, they felt slow to Ngambo.

Every morning he woke up and counted.

“Five days remaining.”

Then:

“Four days remaining.”

Then:

“Three days remaining.”

Finally, the day arrived.

Mother woke him up before sunrise.

“Ngambo.”

He opened his eyes immediately.

Usually Mother had to call him many times before school.

But not today.

Today he jumped out of bed.

“I am ready!”

Mother laughed.

“You have not even washed your face.”

Everyone was busy.

Father checked the documents.

Mother packed food.

His sister helped fold clothes.

Inside the bag went:

shirts

trousers

shoes

books

a small notebook

“Why are you packing a notebook?” his sister asked.

Ngambo smiled.

“I want to write down everything I see.”

Father heard him and smiled.

“That is a good idea.”

“Why?”

“Because memories are treasures. Writing helps us keep them.”

At the Bus Station

Early the next morning, they arrived at the bus station.

Ngambo had never seen so many people travelling at once.

There were families saying goodbye.

There were traders carrying goods.

There were travellers holding bags.

Bus conductors shouted:

“Passengers! Passengers!”

Large buses stood waiting.

Some were going north.

Some were going south.

Some were crossing borders.

Ngambo looked at their bus.

It looked huge.

“Are we really travelling in that?”

His cousin Mwansa laughed.

“What did you expect? A bicycle?”

They both laughed.

Father looked serious.

“Listen carefully.”

The boys became quiet.

“You are travelling far. Always stay together.”

“Yes, Father.”

“Respect everyone you meet.”

“Yes, Father.”

“And remember…”

He paused.

“When you leave Zambia, you represent Zambia.”

Ngambo nodded.

He never forgot those words.

Leaving Home

The bus engine started.

GRRRRRRRRR!

Passengers climbed aboard.

Mother hugged Ngambo tightly.

“Take care of yourself.”

“I will, Mother.”

“And do not lose your bag.”

“I won’t.”

“And eat properly.”

“Yes, Mother.”

“And—”

Father laughed.

“Let the boy travel.”

Everyone smiled.

The bus conductor shouted:

“Ready!”

The doors closed.

Slowly, the bus started moving.

Through the window, Ngambo waved.

His family became smaller.

And smaller.

Until they disappeared.

For a moment, Ngambo felt sad.

Mwansa noticed.

“Missing home already?”

“No.”

“You are.”

“Maybe a little.”

Mwansa smiled.

“That means you have a good home to miss.”

Across Zambia

The journey was long.

Very long.

But Ngambo loved every moment.

Through the window he saw Zambia changing.

Busy towns became quiet villages.

Buildings became forests.

Roads stretched far into the distance.

They passed farmers working in fields.

They saw children waving at the bus.

They crossed bridges.

They saw rivers shining in the sunlight.

Every few hours, the bus stopped.

Passengers stretched their legs.

Women sold:

roasted maize 🌽

groundnuts 🥜

bananas 🍌

drinks

At one stop, an old man sat next to Ngambo.

“Where are you going, young man?”

“Tanzania.”

The old man smiled.

“Ah! A traveller.”

Ngambo laughed.

“It is my first time.”

“Then remember something.”

“What?”

“The world is a big classroom.”

Ngambo wrote those words in his notebook.

The Border

After many hours, the bus finally reached the border.

The driver stood up.

“Everyone prepare your documents.”

Ngambo looked outside.

There were many trucks.

Many buses.

People from different countries.

“This place is busy,” he whispered.

Mwansa nodded.

“This is where countries meet.”

They entered the immigration office.

The officer looked at Ngambo.

“Where are you travelling?”

“Tanzania, Sir.”

“Why?”

“To visit my uncle.”

The officer smiled.

“And what else?”

Ngambo thought.

“To learn.”

The officer laughed.

“That is a good answer.”

STAMP!

His passport was stamped.

He was officially entering another country.

A New Land

When they crossed into Tanzania, Ngambo looked outside carefully.

At first, he laughed.

“It looks the same!”

Mwansa smiled.

“What did you expect?”

“I don’t know.”

“Different trees maybe.”

They both laughed.

But slowly, Ngambo began noticing differences.

Different languages.

Different signs.

Different foods.

Different styles of houses.

He opened his notebook and wrote:

"People may live in different countries, but everywhere people laugh, work, eat, and care for family."

As evening came, the bus continued toward Dar es Salaam.

The stars appeared above.

Most passengers slept.

But Ngambo stayed awake.

Somewhere ahead was:

A new city.

A new culture.

A new adventure.

And for the first time…

The ocean.

Lesson from the Story

Travelling helps us understand the world and respect people from different cultures.

  • Border – a place where one country meets another:

Knowledge Check / Think About It

1. Why was Ngambo excited about travelling?

A. He wanted to avoid meeting anyone new.

B. He was eager to see a new country and learn from the journey.

C. He believed Tanzania was inside his village.

Answer: B

2. What advice did his father give him?

A. Never speak to anyone during the trip.

B. Assume every place is exactly like home.

C. Observe carefully, respect people, and learn from the journey.

Answer: C

3. Why did the old man say the world is a classroom?

A. Travel and experience can teach lessons beyond a classroom.

B. Only buildings can be classrooms.

C. Learning stops when school closes.

Answer: A

4. What can we learn from visiting new places?

A. That every place and person is identical.

B. New cultures, languages, landscapes, and ways of life.

C. That visitors should never ask respectful questions.

Answer: B
