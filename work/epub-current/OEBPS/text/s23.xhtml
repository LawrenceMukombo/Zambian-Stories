CHILDREN OF THE NORTHWEST FOUR

Vumbi and the Dangerous Shortcut

Vumbi ran as though every path were a race and every journey had a trophy waiting at the end.

On the morning of the interschool football match, he promised to meet the team bus at the main road.

A fallen tree delayed traffic, and the coach sent a message saying the bus would wait only fifteen minutes.

"There is a forest shortcut," Vumbi told Chiwape. "We can reach the road before everyone else."

Wana shook his head. "That path is not part of our agreed route."

Vumbi had heard older boys mention it, but he had never walked it himself.

He ran into the trees. Chiwape followed because he did not want his friend to travel alone.

When the Path Disappears

At first, the shortcut seemed clear. Then it divided beside a large anthill.

Vumbi chose the wider branch. Soon thick grass covered their shoes, and neither boy could hear the road.

Chiwape wanted to turn back, but every tree behind them looked the same.

Vumbi pretended not to be worried. "The sun is on that side, so the road must be ahead."

Clouds moved over the sun. They reached a shallow valley filled with wet ground and thorny plants.

Chiwape stopped. "We are lost. We need to use the safety plan from the club."

They stayed together, moved away from the wet ground and returned to the last place they clearly recognised: the anthill.

Chiwape blew the whistle attached to his schoolbag'three short blasts repeated after a pause.

Vumbi placed his bright football shirt on a high branch and resisted the urge to wander in search of help.

The Search

At the main road, Chisola noticed that Vumbi and Chiwape had not arrived.

Wana told the coach about the shortcut. Adults organised a search and contacted the boys' families.

Bernice called their names from the known entrance while an adult followed the path. Chishomyo listened for the whistle.

The answer came faintly through the trees.

The search group found the boys near the anthill. They were tired and embarrassed but safe.

Vumbi apologised for leaving the agreed route and for encouraging Chiwape to follow.

The team missed the first half of the match. Vumbi expected the coach to remove him completely.

Instead, the coach allowed him to sit with the substitutes and watch how the team communicated and followed instructions.

When he entered during the second half, Vumbi passed the ball instead of trying to score alone. Chiwape created the final goal with a careful cross.

Their school did not win, but Vumbi learned something more valuable than the result.

A shortcut is not truly shorter if nobody knows where it leads.

At the next club meeting, he helped create a journey checklist: tell an adult, use agreed routes, travel together, carry a whistle and stop rather than wandering when lost.

Vumbi still loved to run. After that day, he made sure he knew the path before racing along it.

Lesson from the Story

Good preparation and agreed routes are more important than arriving first.

New Words

  • Shortcut: a route intended to reduce travelling distance
  • Recognise: to identify something seen or known before
  • Signal: an action or sound used to communicate
  • Navigate: to find and follow a route

Knowledge Check / Think About It

1. Why did Vumbi enter the forest path?

A. He was collecting mushrooms.

B. The coach ordered him to use it.

C. He wanted to reach the team bus quickly.

Answer: C

2. What did the boys do after realising they were lost?

A. They returned to a known point and signalled.

B. They ran in different directions.

C. They entered the wet valley.

Answer: A

3. How did the search group locate them?

A. They followed football scores.

B. They heard Chiwape's whistle.

C. A bird guided them.

Answer: B

4. Which preparation makes a journey safer?

A. Travel alone without a plan.

B. Hide the destination.

C. Tell adults, use agreed routes and carry a signal.

Answer: C
