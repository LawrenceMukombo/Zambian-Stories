The Fisherman and the Greedy Monkey

A Story About Greed and Contentment

Along the banks of a wide and beautiful river lived an old fisherman named Bwalya.

The river was the heart of the village.

Every morning before sunrise, while the sky was still dark and the air was cool, people began their day near the river.

Women came to collect water.

Children washed before going to school.

Birds flew low above the surface looking for insects.

Fishermen prepared their nets and canoes.

The river gave life to everyone.

And nobody understood the river better than Bwalya.

Bwalya had been fishing since he was a young boy.

His father had taught him.

And his father’s father had taught him before that.

He knew where the biggest fish rested.

He knew when the rains would change the water.

He knew how to listen to nature.

“Fishing,” Bwalya often told the children, “is not only about catching fish.”

The children looked confused.

“Then what else is it about?”

Bwalya smiled.

“It teaches patience.”

The Patient Fisherman

Every morning, Bwalya followed the same routine.

He repaired his fishing net.

He checked his canoe.

Then he paddled quietly into the river.

He never rushed.

Other fishermen sometimes laughed.

“Bwalya, why are you so slow?”

Bwalya smiled.

“The river rewards those who respect it.”

Some fishermen wanted many fish quickly.

They threw their nets everywhere.

They became angry when they caught nothing.

But Bwalya waited.

He observed.

He learned.

And almost every evening, he returned with enough fish.

Not too much.

Not too little.

Enough.

Near the river lived another creature.

Monkey.

Monkey lived in a tall tree overlooking the water.

Every day he watched Bwalya.

He watched him catch fish.

He watched him clean fish.

He watched him cook fish.

And every day Monkey thought the same thing:

“That fish looks delicious.”

Monkey Wants More

One afternoon, Bwalya sat under a tree preparing his meal.

The smell of roasted fish travelled through the air.

Up in the tree, Monkey sniffed.

“Mmmmm.”

He climbed lower.

“Good afternoon, Bwalya.”

The fisherman looked up.

“Good afternoon, Monkey.”

“That fish smells wonderful.”

Bwalya smiled.

“Would you like some?”

Monkey was surprised.

“You will share?”

“Of course.”

Bwalya broke a piece and gave it to him.

Monkey ate happily.

“This is the best fish I have ever tasted!”

Bwalya laughed.

“I am glad you like it.”

Monkey looked at the remaining fish.

Then he looked at Bwalya.

Then he looked at the basket full of fish.

A thought entered his mind.

A greedy thought.

“Why should I only have one piece…”

“…when I can have everything?”

Monkey Makes a Plan

The next morning, Monkey hid in the tree.

He watched Bwalya carefully.

He saw where the fisherman kept his basket.

He saw where he placed his net.

He saw everything.

“If I take the basket,” Monkey whispered, “all the fish will be mine.”

But carrying fish was not easy.

Monkey did not think about that.

Greed often thinks about getting things.

It rarely thinks about the problems afterwards.

When Bwalya walked away to collect firewood, Monkey saw his chance.

Quickly, he climbed down.

He looked left.

He looked right.

Nobody.

He grabbed the basket.

But there was one problem.

The basket was heavy.

Very heavy.

Monkey pulled.

Nothing.

He pulled harder.

Still nothing.

“Why does fish have to weigh so much?” he complained.

Finally, with all his strength, he lifted the basket.

Slowly, he walked away.

Trouble Begins

Monkey wanted to cross a small stream to reach the forest.

He stepped onto a log.

The basket moved.

“Careful,” he told himself.

He took another step.

The basket moved again.

Inside, the fish slipped from side to side.

Monkey became annoyed.

“Stay still, fish!”

But fish do not listen.

Especially stolen fish.

Another step.

Then...

WHOOPS!

Monkey slipped.

SPLASH!

He fell into the water.

The basket opened.

The fish floated away.

Monkey jumped around trying to catch them.

“Come back!”

But the river carried them away.

Bwalya Finds Monkey

Bwalya heard the noise.

“What is happening?”

He ran to the stream.

There he found Monkey.

Wet.

Cold.

Covered in mud.

Holding an empty basket.

Bwalya folded his arms.

“Monkey.”

Monkey looked down.

“Yes?”

“Is that my basket?”

Monkey looked at it.

Then at Bwalya.

Then back at the basket.

“Yes.”

“And were those my fish?”

Monkey spoke quietly.

“Yes.”

Bwalya sat beside him.

“Why?”

Monkey did not answer.

“Yesterday I shared with you.”

Monkey nodded.

“Was it not enough?”

“It was.”

“Then why take everything?”

Monkey looked ashamed.

“Because I wanted more.”

The Lesson by the River

Bwalya picked up a small cup.

He filled it with water.

“Monkey, look at this.”

Monkey looked.

“This cup is full.”

“Yes.”

Bwalya continued pouring water into it.

The water overflowed.

“Why is the water falling?”

“Because the cup already has enough.”

Bwalya nodded.

“A greedy heart is like this cup.”

Monkey listened.

“It does not know when to stop.”

Monkey sat quietly.

For the first time, he understood.

He had been given something freely.

But instead of being thankful…

he wanted everything.

“I am sorry,” Monkey said.

Bwalya smiled.

“Learning from mistakes is better than hiding them.”

A Changed Monkey

The next morning, Bwalya came to the river.

To his surprise, Monkey was waiting.

“What are you doing?”

Monkey pointed to the fishing nets.

“I came to help.”

Bwalya smiled.

Together they repaired the nets.

Together they caught fish.

And when the cooking fire was ready…

Together they shared the meal.

This time Monkey did not ask for everything.

He enjoyed his portion.

Because he finally understood:

Enough can make a person happier than too much.

Many years later, parents in the village still told children:

“Remember Monkey and the fish basket.”

“Do not lose what you have because you are chasing what you do not need.”

Lesson from the Story

Greed can make us lose our blessings. Learning to appreciate what we have brings happiness.

Knowledge Check / Think About It

1. Why did Monkey steal the fish?

A. He was returning the fish to the river.

B. Greed made him want the fisherman?s catch without asking.

C. Bwalya asked him to steal the basket.

Answer: B

2. Why did Bwalya share even after Monkey was wrong?

A. Bwalya wanted Monkey to steal again.

B. Bwalya believed greed should be rewarded.

C. Bwalya chose compassion while still teaching Monkey a lesson.

Answer: C

3. What does it mean to have enough?

A. Being content with what meets your needs instead of always demanding more.

B. Taking everything before someone else can.

C. Refusing every gift or meal.

Answer: A

4. Have you ever wanted more than you needed?

A. Yes, and I can practise being grateful.

B. Sometimes, and I can pause before taking more.

C. Not often, but I can still remember to share.
