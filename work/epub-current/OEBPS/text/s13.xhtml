🦁 CHAPTER THIRTEEN

The Boy Who Cried Lion

A Story About Truth and Trust

A long time ago, in a small village surrounded by wide grasslands, there lived a boy named Mulenga.

The village was a beautiful place.

In the morning, smoke rose gently from cooking fires.

Women prepared breakfast outside their homes.

Men walked to their fields carrying hoes.

Children swept yards before going to school.

And far away beyond the village, cattle and goats grazed peacefully among the tall grass.

The villagers were hardworking people.

They did not have many riches.

They did not have big houses.

But they had something important.

They trusted one another.

Everyone helped everyone.

If one family had a problem, the whole village came together.

The elders always said:

“A village survives because people believe each other.”

But young Mulenga did not yet understand how valuable trust was.

Looking After the Goats

Mulenga’s family owned several goats.

Every morning before school holidays began, his father reminded him:

“Mulenga, during the holiday you have an important responsibility.”

Mulenga smiled.

“What responsibility, Father?”

“You will take the goats to graze.”

Mulenga’s smile disappeared.

“Every day?”

“Yes.”

“But that is boring.”

Father laughed.

“Many important things look boring until you understand why they matter.”

Mulenga looked at the goats.

They just walked.

They ate grass.

They slept under trees.

Nothing exciting ever happened.

At least that is what Mulenga thought.

The next morning, his mother packed food for him.

She gave him:

roasted groundnuts

cassava

a bottle of water

“Be careful,” she said.

“Yes, Mother.”

“Watch the goats.”

“Yes, Mother.”

“And if there is real danger, call for help.”

Mulenga nodded.

Then he led the goats towards the grazing area.

A Boring Afternoon

At first, Mulenga enjoyed himself.

He climbed trees.

He sang songs.

He chased butterflies.

But after some hours…

he became bored.

Very bored.

The goats were quietly eating.

Nothing happened.

Mulenga sat under a tree.

“This is the longest day ever,” he complained.

He looked towards the village.

Then he had an idea.

A bad idea.

A very bad idea.

“I wonder how fast people would come if there was danger.”

He smiled.

He stood up.

Took a deep breath.

And shouted:

“LION!”

“HELP!”

“A LION IS ATTACKING THE GOATS!”

The Village Comes Running

The villagers heard the shouting.

Everyone stopped working.

“A lion?”

“Mulenga is in danger!”

Immediately they ran.

Farmers dropped their hoes.

Women left their cooking.

Young men carried sticks.

Everyone rushed towards the grazing field.

When they arrived, they were breathing heavily.

“Where is the lion?”

“Are you hurt?”

“Where did it go?”

Mulenga looked at their worried faces.

Then he started laughing.

“There is no lion!”

Everyone stared.

“What?”

“I was only joking!”

The villagers were not happy.

An elder stepped forward.

“Mulenga, danger is not something to joke about.”

Mulenga continued smiling.

“But everyone came so quickly!”

“Because we trusted you.”

The elder shook his head.

“Be careful, young man. Trust is easy to lose and difficult to regain.”

The villagers returned home disappointed.

The Second Trick

For a few days, Mulenga behaved.

He watched the goats properly.

But soon, he became bored again.

He remembered how everyone ran.

He laughed to himself.

“They looked so funny.”

He stood up.

“No, I should not.”

He sat down.

Then he stood again.

“Maybe just one last time.”

And again he shouted:

“LION!”

“HELP!”

“THE LION IS BACK!”

The villagers looked up.

Some were unsure.

“Maybe he is joking again.”

“But what if it is real?”

“We cannot leave a child in danger.”

So once more, they ran.

When they arrived…

Mulenga was laughing.

Again.

“No lion!”

“You should have seen your faces!”

This time nobody laughed.

Not one person.

His father looked at him sadly.

That hurt Mulenga more than anger.

“Father?”

His father shook his head.

“My son, today you did not lose goats.”

“You lost something bigger.”

“What?”

“You lost people’s trust.”

A Real Lion Appears

Several days passed.

Mulenga noticed something.

People no longer believed his words quickly.

When he told his friends something, they asked:

“Are you telling the truth?”

Mulenga did not like it.

But he still did not fully understand.

Until one afternoon.

The goats were grazing near some bushes.

Suddenly…

everything became quiet.

The birds stopped singing.

The goats lifted their heads.

Mulenga stood up.

Something was wrong.

Then he heard it.

A deep sound.

“Grrrrrrrr.”

His heart started beating fast.

Slowly, from behind the bushes…

a lion appeared.

A real lion.

Its eyes watched the goats.

Mulenga froze.

Then he shouted:

“LION!”

“HELP!”

“PLEASE HELP!”

“A REAL LION IS HERE!”

Nobody Comes

In the village, people heard him.

One farmer stopped.

“Did you hear Mulenga?”

Another nodded.

“He says there is a lion again.”

They waited.

Nobody moved.

“He fooled us twice.”

“Maybe he wants to laugh at us again.”

Meanwhile, Mulenga shouted louder.

But nobody came.

The lion moved closer.

The goats scattered.

Some ran into the bushes.

Some ran towards the hills.

Mulenga climbed a tree, frightened.

After some time, the lion left.

Learning the Hard Lesson

When Mulenga returned home, he was quiet.

Very quiet.

Some goats were missing.

His father saw his face.

This time he knew.

“You really saw a lion.”

Mulenga nodded.

Tears filled his eyes.

“I told the truth today.”

Father placed a hand on his shoulder.

“I know.”

“But nobody believed me.”

Father nodded.

“Because yesterday’s lies followed today’s truth.”

Mulenga never forgot those words.

Rebuilding Trust

The following weeks were not easy.

Mulenga worked hard.

He apologised to the villagers.

He helped neighbours.

He told the truth even about small things.

Slowly…

very slowly…

people began trusting him again.

One evening, the village elder spoke to him.

“Mulenga, have you learned?”

“Yes.”

“What did you learn?”

Mulenga answered:

“A lie may seem small when you tell it…”

He paused.

“But it grows bigger when you need people to believe you.”

The elder smiled.

The boy had finally understood.

Years later, when Mulenga became a father, he told his children the same lesson.

“Protect your honesty,” he said.

“Because a trusted name is one of the greatest treasures you can own.”

Lesson from the Story

Always tell the truth. Trust takes a long time to build but can be lost quickly through dishonesty.

Knowledge Check / Think About It

1. Why did Mulenga trick the villagers?

A. He was bored and wanted excitement and attention.

B. A lion had appeared both times.

C. The villagers asked him to test them.

Answer: A

2. Why did people stop believing him?

A. They could no longer hear any sound.

B. His repeated lies damaged their trust in his words.

C. They stopped caring about the goats.

Answer: B

3. Can lost trust be repaired?

A. No, trust can never return under any circumstances.

B. Yes, by telling a larger lie.

C. Yes, through honesty, apology, and consistent truthful actions.

Answer: C

4. Why are small lies still dangerous?

A. Small lies weaken trust and can make real warnings ineffective.

B. Small lies always remain harmless.

C. Small lies improve a person?s reputation.

Answer: A
