🐇 CHAPTER TWO

Kalulu and the Greedy Hyena

The night after the great feast at Lion’s home, all the animals in the forest were still talking about Kalulu.

Some animals laughed whenever they remembered how the little hare had entered the feast hidden inside a pot.

Others shook their heads.

“That Kalulu!” said Elephant. “His body is small, but his tricks are bigger than the forest.”

Monkey laughed loudly from the tree.

“If Kalulu’s ears are nearby, keep your food far away!”

Everyone laughed.

Everyone except Hyena.

Hyena sat alone near a dry tree looking unhappy.

Hyena was always hungry.

In the morning, he was hungry.

In the afternoon, he was hungry.

Even after eating, Hyena complained that he was hungry.

“Why does everyone laugh with Kalulu?” Hyena asked himself. “Why does everyone like him? I am bigger. I am stronger. I have sharper teeth.”

Hyena looked at his reflection in a small pool of water.

“I should be the famous one!”

But there was one thing Hyena did not understand.

The animals did not remember Kalulu because he was strong.

They remembered him because he was clever.

The Journey Through the Forest

One hot afternoon, Kalulu decided to visit the other side of the forest.

He packed a small bag with groundnuts and some wild fruits.

As he walked along the dusty path, he heard a voice behind him.

“Kalulu!”

Kalulu stopped.

He knew that voice.

He slowly turned around.

There stood Hyena.

His mouth was open.

His tongue was hanging out.

As usual, he looked hungry.

“Good afternoon, Hyena,” Kalulu greeted him.

Hyena smiled.

“Where are you going, my clever friend?”

Kalulu looked at him carefully.

Whenever Hyena called someone his friend, he normally wanted something.

“I am just taking a walk,” answered Kalulu.

“A walk?” Hyena replied. “Wonderful! I also love walking.”

Kalulu looked at Hyena’s big stomach.

“You love eating more than walking.”

Hyena laughed.

“That is also true.”

The two animals continued along the path.

The sun was hot.

The journey was long.

Soon, both animals became tired.

“I am hungry,” Hyena complained.

Kalulu smiled.

“You were hungry before we started walking.”

“That was morning hunger,” said Hyena.

“What is this one?”

“This is afternoon hunger.”

Kalulu shook his head and laughed.

A Wonderful Smell

After walking for some time, Kalulu suddenly stopped.

His nose moved.

Sniff sniff.

“Hyena,” he whispered.

Hyena looked around.

“What is it? A lion?”

“No.”

“A hunter?”

“No.”

“Then why are you whispering?”

Kalulu smiled.

“I smell food.”

Immediately Hyena jumped up.

“Food?”

“Yes.”

“What kind of food?”

Kalulu closed his eyes and smelled again.

“I smell vegetables.”

Hyena moved closer.

“I smell meat!”

“I smell sweet potatoes,” said Kalulu.

“I smell everything!” shouted Hyena.

Without waiting, Hyena ran toward the smell.

“Wait!” called Kalulu.

But Hyena was already gone.

The Hidden Cooking Pot

Behind some bushes, the two animals found a large cooking pot.

A farmer who was working nearby had prepared his meal and left it there while he went to collect firewood.

Inside the pot was a delicious stew.

There were sweet potatoes.

There were vegetables.

There was meat.

Hyena’s eyes became big.

His mouth started watering.

“Kalulu,” he said slowly.

“Yes, my friend?”

“I have an idea.”

Kalulu folded his arms.

Whenever Hyena had an idea, it was usually a bad one.

“What idea?”

Hyena pointed at himself.

“Since I found the food first, I should eat first.”

Kalulu looked surprised.

“You found it first?”

“Yes.”

“But I smelled it first.”

Hyena thought for a moment.

“That is true. Then we should share.”

Kalulu smiled.

For once, Hyena was making sense.

“That is fair.”

But then Hyena continued.

“We shall share like this. I will eat the meat and sweet potatoes. You can drink the soup.”

Kalulu stared at him.

“Only the soup?”

“Yes,” Hyena replied proudly. “Because I am bigger.”

Kalulu sat quietly.

He knew arguing with greedy Hyena was a waste of time.

So he began thinking.

And whenever Kalulu started thinking...

A trick was coming.

Kalulu’s Clever Plan

“My friend Hyena,” Kalulu finally said.

“You are right.”

Hyena smiled.

“I am?”

“Yes. You are bigger and stronger.”

Hyena stood proudly.

“Exactly.”

“So,” continued Kalulu, “you should eat first.”

Hyena could not believe his luck.

“You mean it?”

“Yes.”

Hyena rushed toward the pot.

“But wait,” said Kalulu.

Hyena stopped.

“What now?”

“Someone must watch for danger.”

Hyena looked around nervously.

“Danger?”

“Yes. What if the farmer comes back?”

Hyena stepped away from the pot.

“I did not think about that.”

Kalulu pointed to a small hill.

“I will climb up there and watch. If I see the farmer, I will warn you.”

Hyena smiled.

“Kalulu, sometimes you are a very good friend.”

Kalulu climbed the hill.

Hyena sat next to the pot.

He picked up the first piece of meat.

Before he could eat it...

Kalulu shouted:

“HYENA!”

Hyena jumped.

“What?”

“He is coming!”

“Who?”

“The farmer!”

Hyena screamed.

“Aaaaaah!”

He dropped the meat and ran into the bushes.

Kalulu came down slowly.

He looked around.

No farmer.

He sat near the pot.

“Well,” he said, “it is wrong to waste good food.”

And he started eating.

Hyena Returns

After hiding for a long time, Hyena became confused.

“Why has the farmer not passed?”

Slowly, he walked back.

When he reached the pot, he found Kalulu sitting happily.

The pot was almost empty.

“Kalulu!”

Kalulu looked up.

“Oh, Hyena. You came back.”

“You tricked me!”

Kalulu wiped his mouth.

“No, my friend. Your greed tricked you.”

Hyena became quiet.

Kalulu continued.

“If you had agreed to share fairly, we both would have eaten together.”

Hyena looked down.

He knew Kalulu was right.

“I wanted everything,” Hyena admitted.

“And what did you get?”

Hyena looked inside the pot.

Only a tiny piece of sweet potato remained.

“Almost nothing,” he whispered.

Kalulu pushed the piece toward him.

“Here, my friend.”

Hyena looked surprised.

“You are sharing after I was greedy?”

“Yes,” said Kalulu. “Because everyone deserves a chance to learn.”

Hyena ate the small piece slowly.

For the first time, he enjoyed a small meal more than a big one.

Because this meal came with wisdom.

Lesson from the Story

Greed can make us lose what we already have. Sharing brings friendship and happiness.

New Words

  • Fair – treating everyone equally:
  • Share – giving part of something to others:
  • Wisdom – knowing what is right:

Knowledge Check / Think About It

1. Why did Hyena want more food than Kalulu?

A. Greed made Hyena want the largest share for himself.

B. Kalulu had already eaten all the food.

C. Hyena was collecting food for the whole forest.

Answer: A

2. Did Kalulu teach Hyena a good lesson?

A. No. Hyena learned that sharing is dangerous.

B. Yes. He showed Hyena that greed can make a person lose what sharing would save.

C. No. Kalulu taught Hyena to hide food better.

Answer: B

3. Why is sharing important among friends?

A. Sharing makes food disappear faster.

B. Friends should compete for every meal.

C. Sharing strengthens friendship and helps everyone have enough.

Answer: C
