CHILDREN OF THE NORTHWEST TWO

Bernice and the Storytelling Day

Bernice could turn an ordinary classroom into a moonlit village with only her voice.

When she described wind, listeners heard leaves rustling. When she imitated a proud chief or a frightened goat, even the teachers laughed.

The school announced a storytelling day. Each pupil could present a family story, an original tale or an account of local history.

Bernice wanted to win the audience prize. She remembered a beautiful story her grandmother had told beside the evening fire.

"I will perform Grandmother's story," she told Chisola. "Everyone will love it."

"Have you asked whether she wants it shared?" Chisola asked.

Bernice had not considered that question.

Asking Before Telling

That evening, Bernice visited her grandmother and repeated the story's opening lines.

Her grandmother smiled, then became thoughtful. She explained that parts of the story belonged to their family, while another part had been learned from an older storyteller many years earlier.

"A story may travel," Grandmother said, "but a careful storyteller remembers the road it travelled."

She allowed Bernice to share the family section if she introduced its source. She asked her not to perform a short ceremonial song because Bernice did not yet understand its purpose.

Bernice felt disappointed. The song was the most dramatic part.

Grandmother offered another path. "Create your own ending. Let the old story give you a seed, but grow something honest from your imagination."

Building an Original Ending

The next afternoon, the friends helped Bernice practise.

Wana listened for confusing parts. Chawana suggested that the lost child follow fireflies home. Vumbi wanted a chase through the forest. Chishomyo proposed a clever signal made with reflected moonlight.

Bernice combined the ideas but wrote the final words herself.

At storytelling day, she stood before pupils, parents and elders.

"The beginning of this story was shared with me by my grandmother," she said. "I am presenting it with her permission. The ending is my own."

She told of a child lost between tall trees, guided not by magic alone but by lessons remembered from home.

When she reached the omitted song, Bernice did not imitate it. She paused, described the respectful silence around it and moved into her new ending.

The room remained completely still until her final sentence. Then applause filled the hall.

Another pupil received the audience prize, but the elders gave Bernice special recognition for responsible storytelling.

Her grandmother hugged her. "Today you did more than entertain people. You respected the story and those who carried it."

Bernice later started a source page in her notebook. For every story, she recorded who shared it, whether she had permission and which parts she created.

She discovered that giving credit did not make her voice smaller. It made her storytelling more trustworthy.

Lesson from the Story

Stories should be shared with permission, honesty and respect for their sources.

New Words

  • Source: the person or place from which information comes
  • Permission: agreement allowing someone to do something
  • Ceremonial: connected with a formal cultural occasion
  • Credit: public recognition of someone's contribution

Knowledge Check / Think About It

1. Why did Chisola advise Bernice to speak to her grandmother?

A. The story had sources and permissions to consider.

B. Bernice could not speak loudly.

C. The event prohibited family stories.

Answer: A

2. Why was the ceremonial song omitted?

A. The song was too short.

B. Bernice did not understand its ceremonial purpose.

C. Nobody remembered the tune.

Answer: B

3. How did Bernice make the performance partly original?

A. She claimed the entire old story.

B. She copied another pupil.

C. She wrote a new ending and identified it as her own.

Answer: C

4. What should a storyteller do before sharing a family story publicly?

A. Ask permission and credit the source.

B. Remove the source's name.

C. Pretend it is entirely new.

Answer: A
