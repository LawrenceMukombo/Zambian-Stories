👑 CHAPTER TWELVE

The King Who Had No Child

A Story About Honesty and Integrity

Many, many years ago, beyond the valleys and beyond the great rivers, there was a beautiful kingdom.

The land was rich.

The rivers were full of fish.

The fields produced plenty of food.

The forests provided fruits and medicine.

The people lived peacefully.

At the centre of the kingdom stood a magnificent palace.

It was not the size of the palace that made it special.

It was not the gold decorations.

It was not the tall walls.

It was the person who lived inside.

The king.

His name was King Mulenga.

King Mulenga was loved by his people.

Every morning, he left the palace and walked among them.

He visited farmers.

He listened to traders.

He talked with children.

He sat with elders.

Many kings wanted people to serve them.

But King Mulenga believed something different.

“A good leader,” he always said, “must first learn how to serve.”

Because of this, his people respected him.

Whenever there was a problem, they trusted his judgement.

Whenever there was disagreement, they trusted his fairness.

King Mulenga had almost everything a person could wish for.

But deep inside his heart…

there was sadness.

The Empty Chair

Inside the palace was a special room.

In that room were two beautiful chairs.

One belonged to the king.

The other chair was smaller.

It had carefully carved animals on it.

A lion.

An elephant.

A bird.

A hare.

Many years earlier, the chair had been made for the king’s future child.

But years passed.

The chair remained empty.

The king had no son.

He had no daughter.

No child would inherit the throne.

Sometimes the king entered the room alone.

He touched the empty chair and sighed.

“My kingdom has many riches,” he whispered.

“But who will care for my people when I am gone?”

Searching for an Answer

One evening, King Mulenga called his advisers.

They were the wisest people in the kingdom.

“My friends,” said the king, “I am growing old.”

The advisers became silent.

“I must choose someone who will lead this kingdom after me.”

One adviser stood.

“Your Majesty, choose the strongest warrior.”

The king shook his head.

“Strength alone cannot rule people.”

Another adviser spoke.

“Choose the richest person.”

The king shook his head again.

“Wealth does not always bring wisdom.”

A third adviser said:

“Choose the cleverest person.”

The king thought carefully.

“Cleverness without honesty can become dangerous.”

Everyone became quiet.

Finally, the oldest adviser stood.

His hair was white.

His walking stick was old.

But his mind was sharp.

“Your Majesty.”

“Yes?”

“A great leader must have one thing above everything.”

“What?”

“Integrity.”

The king nodded slowly.

“How do we find such a person?”

The old adviser smiled.

“Test their honesty when nobody is watching.”

The King’s Announcement

The next morning, drums sounded throughout the kingdom.

BOOM! BOOM! BOOM!

The royal messenger walked from village to village.

“Listen! Listen!”

“The king has an announcement!”

People gathered.

The messenger opened a scroll.

“Our great king wishes to choose a child who may one day become ruler.”

Everyone gasped.

A child?

The messenger continued:

“All children between ten and fifteen years old must come to the palace tomorrow.”

“The king will give each child a special seed.”

“The child who grows the best plant will be chosen.”

Excitement filled the kingdom.

Parents talked.

Children dreamed.

Everyone wanted to win.

A Poor Boy Named Liyungu

Far away from the palace lived a boy named Liyungu.

Liyungu lived with his mother in a small house.

They were not rich.

They did not own many things.

But his mother taught him important lessons.

“My son,” she often said, “a good name is worth more than gold.”

Liyungu worked hard.

He helped neighbours.

He respected elders.

When he made mistakes, he told the truth.

When he found things that did not belong to him, he returned them.

His mother was proud.

When Liyungu heard about the king’s challenge, he became excited.

“Mother, may I try?”

His mother smiled.

“Of course.”

“But many children will have better pots.”

“That does not matter.”

“They may have better gardens.”

“That does not matter.”

“Then what matters?”

His mother touched his chest.

“What you carry inside.”

The Seeds Are Given

The next morning, hundreds of children gathered at the palace.

Some wore expensive clothes.

Some carried beautiful pots.

Others talked proudly.

“I will win.”

“My father is the best farmer.”

“My plant will be taller than everyone’s.”

Liyungu stood quietly.

Finally, King Mulenga appeared.

He carried a basket of seeds.

One by one, each child received a seed.

The king said:

“Care for your seed.”

“After three months, return with what you have grown.”

The children bowed and went home.

The Empty Pot

As soon as Liyungu reached home, he began working.

He found good soil.

He prepared his pot.

He planted the seed carefully.

Every morning:

He watered it.

Every afternoon:

He checked it.

Every evening:

He moved it away from danger.

One week passed.

Nothing.

Two weeks.

Nothing.

One month.

Still nothing.

Liyungu became worried.

Meanwhile, other children started showing their plants.

“My plant already has leaves!”

“Mine is growing taller!”

“Mine has flowers!”

Liyungu looked at his empty pot.

He felt embarrassed.

“What am I doing wrong?”

He changed the soil.

He used a different pot.

He tried everything.

But nothing grew.

The Day Arrives

After three months, the palace gates opened.

Children arrived carrying beautiful plants.

Some plants were tall.

Some had colourful flowers.

Some had large green leaves.

People cheered.

“Look at that one!”

“Surely that child will win!”

Then Liyungu arrived.

Carrying...

an empty pot.

People stared.

Some children laughed.

“Why did you come?”

“You grew nothing!”

“Go home!”

Liyungu wanted to run away.

But his mother’s words returned:

“A good name is worth more than gold.”

So he stayed.

The King Inspects the Plants

King Mulenga walked slowly.

He looked at every plant.

But strangely…

he did not smile.

The children became confused.

Finally, he reached Liyungu.

He stopped.

“What is your name?”

“Liyungu, Your Majesty.”

The king looked at the empty pot.

“Where is your plant?”

Liyungu looked down.

“I failed.”

The crowd became silent.

“I watered the seed.”

“I cared for it.”

“I tried my best.”

“But nothing grew.”

The king looked into his eyes.

“Are you telling the truth?”

“Yes, Your Majesty.”

The Surprise

Suddenly…

the king smiled.

A big smile.

He lifted Liyungu’s hand.

Everyone was shocked.

“My people!”

The crowd listened.

“I have found the next ruler.”

People whispered.

“How?”

“His pot is empty!”

The king raised his hand.

“Three months ago, I gave every child a seed.”

He paused.

“But those seeds were boiled.”

The crowd gasped.

“Boiled seeds cannot grow.”

The children looked at their plants.

The king continued:

“Everyone who brought a plant replaced my seed.”

“But Liyungu brought the truth.”

The kingdom became silent.

A New Leader

Years passed.

King Mulenga trained Liyungu.

He taught him leadership.

He taught him patience.

He taught him wisdom.

And when Liyungu finally became king, the people loved him.

Because they knew something important:

A person who can be trusted with a small seed…

can be trusted with a great kingdom.

Lesson from the Story

Honesty is valuable even when dishonesty appears successful. True character is shown when telling the truth is difficult.

  • Successor – someone who takes another person’s place:
  • Honesty – telling and living the truth:
  • Character – the qualities that show who someone really is:

Knowledge Check / Think About It

1. Why did the king boil the seeds?

A. To make the seeds grow faster.

B. To choose the tallest plant.

C. To test which child would remain honest when nothing grew.

Answer: C

2. Why did other children replace their seeds?

A. They feared bringing empty pots and wanted to appear successful.

B. They did not know plants needed water.

C. The king told them to buy new seeds.

Answer: A

3. Was Liyungu brave?

A. No. Honesty never requires courage.

B. Yes. He presented the empty pot even though he feared embarrassment.

C. No. He should have hidden from the king.

Answer: B

4. Would you carry the empty pot to the palace?

A. No, because an impressive lie is safer.

B. Only if every other child also had an empty pot.

C. Yes, because honesty matters more than appearances.

Answer: C
