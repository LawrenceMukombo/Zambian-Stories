ORIGINAL STORY ONE

Jelita and the Missing Library Book

Jelita loved Wednesdays because Wednesday was library day at Chisomo Primary School.

The library occupied one bright room beside the head teacher's office. It was not large, but to Jelita it felt bigger than the whole town. Its shelves opened doors to distant kingdoms, deep forests, great rivers and planets that shone beyond the night sky.

Her favourite book was The Moon over the Zambezi. It told the story of two children who followed the silver path of moonlight across the river to help a lost fisherman find his village.

Jelita had borrowed it twice. On Tuesday afternoon, she returned it through the wooden return box before hurrying to assembly.

On Wednesday morning, Mrs Phiri stood beside the library door with a worried expression.

"Before anyone borrows a book today, I need your help," she said. "The Moon over the Zambezi is missing. It was recorded as returned, but it is not on its shelf."

Jelita's stomach tightened. "I returned it yesterday, Mrs Phiri. I placed it in the box myself."

Mulenga, who had come to look for a book about bridges, noticed her concern. "Then the book must still be somewhere nearby," he said. "Let us help search."

Following the Clues

Mrs Phiri agreed, but reminded them to handle every book carefully and return each one to its correct place.

Jelita searched the story shelves. Mulenga examined the return box and looked behind the librarian's desk. They checked under chairs, between encyclopaedias and inside a cupboard of old newspapers.

The book was nowhere to be seen.

Then Jelita noticed a thin trail of red dust. It began beside the return box and crossed the floor toward the reading corner.

"This dust came from outside," she said. "Someone with dusty shoes walked from the box to the atlases."

Mulenga lifted the heavy atlases one at a time. Beneath the last book, he found a torn borrowing card.

"Now we have a clue," he whispered. "But where is the book?"

A younger pupil named Tisa stood at the doorway. She held her schoolbag tightly and looked as though she wanted to speak, but every time Mrs Phiri glanced toward her, she lowered her eyes.

Jelita walked over without accusing her. "Hello, Tisa. Are you looking for a book?"

Tisa shook her head. "I came because? because I know something about the missing one."

They moved to a quiet corner. Tisa explained that she had found The Moon over the Zambezi inside the return box after assembly.

"I wanted to read it," she said. "But I was afraid Mrs Phiri would refuse. I still owe a fine because my little brother drew in another library book."

She had hidden the book behind the atlases, planning to ask about it later. When she returned that morning, it was gone.

Mulenga frowned. "Moving it without permission caused this confusion."

Jelita nodded, but her voice remained gentle. "Thank you for telling us. Now we can solve the problem together."

Telling the Whole Truth

The three pupils told Mrs Phiri everything. Tisa's words came slowly at first, then rushed out all at once.

Mrs Phiri was disappointed, but she did not shout. "A mistake becomes harder to repair when we hide it," she said. "Telling the truth gives us somewhere to begin."

They returned to the reading corner. Mulenga searched beneath the woven mats. Jelita checked behind the low shelf. Mrs Phiri examined the windowsill.

A sudden breeze lifted several worksheets from a table and carried them through the open window.

Jelita looked outside. Pages lay against the fence, and one white sheet had caught beneath a flowering shrub.

"The wind!" she cried. "If the book was left near this window, perhaps the wind pushed it too."

They hurried outside. Beyond the shrub, hidden by long leaves, lay The Moon over the Zambezi. Its cover was dusty, but its pages were dry and undamaged.

Everyone sighed with relief.

Repairing the Mistake

Tisa apologised for moving the book and promised always to ask before borrowing.

Mrs Phiri explained that the damaged-book fine was meant to teach care, not to stop a child from reading. She arranged for Tisa to help dust shelves and repair book covers for twenty minutes each Friday until the fine was settled.

"You will still be allowed to borrow one book at a time," Mrs Phiri added, ?as long as you care for it responsibly."

Tisa's face brightened. She filled in a borrowing card and officially checked out The Moon over the Zambezi.

Before leaving, Jelita helped Mrs Phiri place a secure lid on the return box and move it away from the open window. Mulenga made a small sign reminding readers to return books properly.

That afternoon, Tisa sat beneath a jacaranda tree reading the opening page. Jelita and Mulenga passed her on their way home.

"Mystery solved," Mulenga said proudly.

Jelita smiled. Finding the book felt good, but helping someone admit a mistake and remain welcome in the library felt even better.

Lesson from the Story

Honesty and kindness make it easier to correct mistakes.

New Words

  • Library: a place where books are kept for reading or borrowing
  • Clue: something that helps solve a mystery
  • Admit: to tell the truth about something
  • Relief: comfort felt when worry has ended
