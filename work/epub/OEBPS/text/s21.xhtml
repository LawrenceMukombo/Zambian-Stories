The Hunter and the Talking Drum

Mukuka the hunter found a talking drum hidden beneath a tree where the forest grew thick and cool. The drum's voice was low and patient. 'Play me gently,' it said, 'and I will bring what you need. Play me harshly, and you will meet misfortune.' Mukuka tapped the drum with respect. Fresh fruit appeared, then honey, then warm food wrapped in leaves. He thanked the drum and ate enough for his journey. For several days he played gently. Then greed began whispering in his ear. 'Why ask for food,' it said, 'when you could ask for gold?' Mukuka beat the drum faster and harder. 'Bring riches!' he demanded. The drum cracked. A strong wind burst through the trees, scattering his arrows, his basket, and his pride. Mukuka fell backward into the leaves and lay there listening to the silence he had caused. He repaired the drum with care and played a soft song of apology. The drum never gave him gold, but it gave him what he needed, and Mukuka learned to respect gifts instead of squeezing them dry.

Moral

Greed can break a blessing.
