Kalulu and the Tortoise Race

Kalulu challenged Tortoise to a race through the forest. The path twisted past the anthill, under the sausage tree, over a dry streambed, and ended beside a smooth stone where the animals would wait. 'I am so fast,' Kalulu boasted, 'that I can sleep before you arrive.' Tortoise only smiled. 'Then run as you wish, my friend. I will walk as I can.' When the race began, Kalulu flew down the path like a thrown seed. Dust rose behind him. Birds called his name. Near the finish line he looked back and saw no Tortoise at all. 'A small nap will not hurt,' he said, curling beneath a shady tree. Tortoise kept moving. Step by step, he passed the anthill. Step by step, he crossed the dry streambed. The sun moved across the sky, but Tortoise did not complain and did not stop. By evening, the animals cheered as Tortoise crossed the finish line. Kalulu woke to the sound and stared in surprise. Tortoise said gently, 'Fast feet are useful, but a steady heart reaches the end.' Kalulu bowed and never laughed at slow steps again.

Moral

Steady effort can outrun careless confidence.
