Kalulu and the Farmer's Trap

A farmer who was tired of losing sweet potatoes set a basket trap at the edge of his field. He covered it with leaves and placed one bright potato beside it. From the bushes, Monkey laughed. 'Only a foolish animal would step there.' Kalulu heard him and held up another sweet potato. 'Then you must be the bravest animal in the field,' he said. Monkey puffed out his chest. He loved being called brave almost as much as he loved sweet potatoes. Monkey strutted toward the biggest leaves. 'Watch me,' he said. The moment his foot touched the trap, the basket dropped over him with a hollow thump. Monkey shouted, rattled the basket, and forgot all his proud words. Kalulu called the farmer instead of running away. The farmer lifted the basket and looked at both animals. 'My field feeds my family,' he said. 'Taking without asking brings trouble.' Monkey apologized. Kalulu admitted that he had tempted Monkey with praise. The farmer gave each of them one small sweet potato for telling the truth. As they walked home, Monkey said, 'Next time I will listen to my eyes, not my pride.' Kalulu nodded, because even a trickster can learn from a trap.

Moral

Pride can walk straight into a trap.
