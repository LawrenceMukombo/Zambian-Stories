The Clever Hare and the Crocodile

One morning Chifundo the Clever Hare wanted to cross the great Lunsemfwa River. On the far bank grew a field of sweet potatoes, and Chifundo could almost taste them whenever the wind blew across the water. The river, however, belonged to crocodiles. Their eyes floated like little stones, and their tails moved beneath the surface. Chifundo knew that if he tried to swim, he would become their breakfast before reaching the middle. He called out, 'Crocodiles! The king of the forest has sent me with an important duty.' The largest crocodile lifted his head. 'What duty?' Chifundo answered, 'I must count all loyal river subjects before sunset.' The crocodiles were proud to be counted. They lined up from one bank to the other, nose to tail, making a living bridge. Chifundo stepped carefully from back to back, counting in a loud, serious voice. When he reached the field, he bowed and said, 'The king will be pleased to know you are many.' Then he vanished among the leaves. The crocodiles grumbled, but Chifundo had already learned that a brave plan needs a calm voice.

Moral

Intelligence and wit can overcome dangerous obstacles.
