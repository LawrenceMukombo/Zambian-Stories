The Girl Who Could Talk to Birds

Mubanga had a gift no one else in the village understood. When birds sang, she heard more than music. She heard warnings, jokes, directions, and tiny stories about the world above the trees. Some children teased her for listening to sparrows, but Mubanga did not mind. She knew the weaver birds could tell when rain was coming, and the doves knew which paths were quiet. One morning a small sparrow landed near her window, chirping urgently. 'Danger in the grain store,' it said. 'A snake is hiding where your family keeps the maize.' Mubanga ran to her parents. They believed her because she had never used her gift for foolishness. Carefully they opened the grain store and found a poisonous snake coiled in the corner. The elders removed it safely before anyone was hurt. From then on, the village listened when Mubanga listened. She taught the children that nature speaks in many voices, and wisdom often arrives with wings.

Moral

Nature has wisdom for those who listen.
