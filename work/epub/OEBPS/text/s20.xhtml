The King's Three Questions

A wise king ruled a peaceful land, yet three questions troubled him. What is the most important time? Who is the most important person? What is the most important action? Scholars arrived with scrolls. Warriors arrived with stories. Traders arrived with clever sayings. The king listened to them all, but his heart remained unsatisfied. One day he walked beyond the palace and found an old farmer planting seeds. The farmer's hands were rough, and his back was bent, but his face was peaceful. The king asked his questions. The farmer pressed a seed into the soil. 'The most important time is now,' he said, 'because it is the only time in your hands. The most important person is the one beside you, because that is the person you can care for. The most important action is to do good, because goodness keeps life growing.' The king returned to the palace changed. He listened more closely, helped more quickly, and ruled as if every ordinary moment could become a seed.

Moral

The present moment, kindness, and care are great treasures.
