The Boy Who Cried Lion

In the village of Mpongwe, Mulenga was trusted to watch the goats. Each morning he led them to the grazing hill and sat beneath a tree while they nibbled grass. The work was important, but Mulenga thought it was dull. One day he ran back to the village shouting, 'Lion! Lion!' Mothers dropped baskets, fathers grabbed sticks, and elders hurried up the hill. When they arrived, the goats were safe and Mulenga was laughing. The elder warned him, 'A lie is a crack in a clay pot. Make too many cracks and no one can carry water for you.' Mulenga nodded, but a few days later he played the trick again. Then, one hot afternoon, a real lion stepped from the bushes. Mulenga's laughter vanished. He ran and shouted until his throat hurt, but the villagers remembered his tricks and did not come quickly. The lion chased away several goats before the men arrived. Mulenga was safe, but ashamed. From that day, when he spoke, he made sure his words were strong enough to stand on truth.

Moral

Honesty builds trust.
