Kalulu and the Lion's Feast

Lion once announced a grand feast beneath the tallest trees in the forest. All morning the animals swept the clearing, laid out woven mats, and filled baskets with mangoes, bananas, groundnuts, and roasted maize. At the very center of the table sat a shiny honey pot, golden and sweet, guarded by Lion's proudest stare. Kalulu the Hare had not been invited. The animals loved his jokes, but they also knew that his jokes often ended with someone missing a meal. Still, when the smell of honey floated between the trees, Kalulu's nose twitched and his feet began to move before his good sense could catch up. He crept behind Elephant, ducked under Giraffe's long shadow, and slipped into the honey pot. Only his long ears poked above the rim. Zebra blinked twice and whispered, 'That honey pot has grown ears.' Soon every animal was staring. Lion roared, 'Who is hiding at my feast?' Kalulu sprang out, sticky from whiskers to tail, and bowed so low his ears touched the table. 'Your Majesty,' he said, 'I came to guard the honey from thieves.' For a moment the clearing was silent. Then Elephant laughed, and Giraffe laughed, and at last even Lion laughed. Lion gave Kalulu a small bowl of honey, but he also gave him a lesson. 'A clever tongue may open a door,' Lion said, 'but an honest heart is welcome at the table.' Kalulu licked the honey slowly and promised that next time he would ask before he sneaked.

Moral

Cleverness is best when it is honest.
