Kalulu and the Talking Drum

Near a village by the river, Kalulu found a small drum resting in the shade of a fig tree. Its skin was smooth, its wooden sides were carved with tiny patterns, and when the wind touched it, the drum hummed as if it were remembering an old song. 'Play gently,' said a voice from inside the drum, 'and gifts will appear. Play proudly, and trouble will follow.' Kalulu's eyes grew wide. He tapped the drum with two careful paws. At once, oranges rolled from the grass, maize cakes appeared on a mat, and children came running with happy cries. For a while Kalulu played softly, and everyone shared. Then the praise began to tickle him. 'Kalulu is wonderful!' shouted one child. 'Kalulu is magical!' said another. Kalulu lifted his chin and beat the drum harder. He sang the forbidden song, the one that said the drum belonged only to him. The music stopped. A puff of dust leapt from the drum and covered his nose. The chief, who had been watching, raised one quiet eyebrow. Kalulu lowered his head and wiped the dust from his whiskers. This time he played a song of thanks. The drum answered with enough food for the whole village, and Kalulu helped carry baskets to the elders before taking any for himself. He learned that magic grows smaller in proud hands and larger in generous ones.

Moral

Magic works best in humble hands.
