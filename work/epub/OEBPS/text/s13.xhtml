The Fisherman and the Greedy Monkey

Bwalya the fisherman rose before sunrise and walked to the river with his basket and traps. He knew where the water ran deep, where the reeds hid small fish, and where the morning mist lifted first. One day his basket filled quickly. Misozi the Monkey watched from a branch, his eyes round with greed. 'Friend Fisherman,' he called, 'you look tired. Let me hold that basket while you rest.' Bwalya knew Monkey's tricks, but he also wanted to teach him. He set the basket down. The moment Bwalya turned away, Misozi grabbed it. The basket was heavier than he expected. He staggered, slipped on the muddy bank, and splashed into the river. Bwalya pulled him out by the arm. Misozi coughed, shivered, and looked at the fish scattered near the reeds. 'Greed nearly carried you away,' Bwalya said. 'A hungry mouth should ask, not steal.' Misozi apologized. Bwalya gave him one fish and kept the rest for market. Monkey ate slowly that day, tasting both the fish and the lesson.

Moral

Greed often leads to a fall.
