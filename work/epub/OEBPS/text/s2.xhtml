Kalulu and the Hyena's Greed

One dry afternoon, Hyena found a clay pot of stew bubbling in a quiet forest clearing. The stew smelled of pumpkin leaves, groundnuts, and warm spices. Hyena looked left, looked right, and grinned. 'All mine,' he said, though the pot was large enough to feed many friends. Kalulu watched from a little hill. He had been hungry since sunrise, but he also knew Hyena's greedy ways. 'Good afternoon, Hyena,' he called. 'That stew looks heavy for one stomach.' Hyena hugged the pot and snapped, 'Then it is lucky I have a very large stomach.' Kalulu placed one paw above his eyes as if he saw something far away. 'Oh dear,' he shouted. 'A hunter is coming this way with a net and a sharp spear!' Hyena forgot the stew, forgot his pride, and ran so fast that dust covered the bushes. Kalulu sat by the pot and stirred it carefully so it would not burn. When Hyena crept back, ashamed and still hungry, Kalulu did not eat everything. He handed Hyena a bowl and said, 'Fear made you drop what greed would not share.' Hyena's ears drooped. The two ate together under the trees, and the stew tasted better than it would have tasted alone. From that day, Hyena still loved food, but he learned to call a friend before lifting the lid.

Moral

Greed makes us lose what sharing could have saved.
