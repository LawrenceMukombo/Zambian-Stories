Kalulu and the Crocodile Bridge

Kalulu stood on the bank of the wide river and stared at the sweet potato field on the other side. The leaves were green, the soil was soft, and the potatoes underneath were surely fat and sweet. Between Kalulu and his supper, however, lay deep water full of crocodiles. He could not swim across. He could not jump across. So he sat down, scratched one ear, and let his clever thoughts walk around in a circle. Soon he stood up and shouted, 'River guards! The forest king has sent me to count his strongest subjects.' The crocodiles rose from the water one by one. They liked being called guards, and they liked being counted even more. The largest crocodile said, 'Begin with me.' Kalulu shook his head. 'The king requires a proper line from this bank to that bank.' Proudly the crocodiles stretched across the river. Kalulu hopped from back to back, counting loudly, 'One, two, three, four!' He kept his feet light and his smile polite. When he reached the far bank, he bowed. 'Thank you for the bridge,' he called, then disappeared into the sweet potato leaves. The crocodiles splashed and grumbled, but even they had to admit that Kalulu's mind had crossed the river before his feet did.

Moral

A quick mind can cross a wide river.
