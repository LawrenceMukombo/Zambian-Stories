🐇 CHAPTER FOUR

Kalulu and the Farmer’s Trap

The rainy season had ended.

The heavy clouds that once covered the sky had disappeared, and the warm sun now shone brightly over the land.

Everywhere you looked, the world was full of life.

The grass was green.

The rivers were full.

The birds sang from the trees.

And the farmers smiled as they looked across their fields.

It had been a good farming season.

In one village near the edge of the forest lived an old farmer named Ba Simukonda.

Everyone knew Ba Simukonda.

He was a hardworking man.

Every morning before sunrise, while most people were still sleeping, Ba Simukonda was already awake.

He sharpened his hoe.

He checked his tools.

Then he walked to his garden.

His garden was famous.

People travelled from nearby villages just to see it.

There were rows of maize standing tall.

There were pumpkins lying on the ground.

There were tomatoes shining red in the sunlight.

And best of all…

There were sweet potatoes.

Big.

Fresh.

Delicious.

Sweet potatoes.

Unfortunately, someone else also knew about the sweet potatoes.

Kalulu.

The Missing Sweet Potatoes

One morning, Ba Simukonda entered his garden carrying his basket.

He was happy.

“Today I will harvest some sweet potatoes for my grandchildren,” he said.

He walked to the sweet potato field.

Then he stopped.

Something was wrong.

Very wrong.

There was a hole in the ground.

One plant had been pulled out.

Then another.

Then another.

“My sweet potatoes!”

Ba Simukonda looked carefully.

On the soft ground were tiny footprints.

Small footprints.

Rabbit footprints.

The farmer folded his arms.

“Kalulu.”

He knew immediately.

“This little hare thinks he is clever.”

The farmer shook his head.

“But this time, I will catch him.”

Kalulu Returns

That evening, when the moon appeared, two long ears appeared from behind a bush.

It was Kalulu.

He looked left.

He looked right.

Nobody.

“Wonderful,” whispered Kalulu.

“Everyone is sleeping.”

Slowly, he entered the garden.

He pulled out one sweet potato.

“Ah!”

He smiled.

“This farmer grows the best sweet potatoes in the whole land.”

He pulled another one.

And another.

And another.

Suddenly he stopped.

“What if the farmer notices?”

Kalulu looked at the empty spaces.

Then he laughed.

“Of course he will notice.”

He picked up another sweet potato.

“But by then I will be far away.”

And he disappeared into the forest.

The Farmer Makes a Plan

The next morning, Ba Simukonda returned.

More sweet potatoes were gone.

This time he did not get angry.

He smiled.

“Kalulu likes tricks.”

He picked up some wood.

Some rope.

And a large basket.

“Today,” he said, “I will also make a trick.”

The farmer worked quietly.

By sunset, the trap was ready.

A large basket was placed above the ground.

Under the basket he placed the biggest sweet potato from the field.

Anyone who touched it…

SNAP!

The basket would fall.

Monkey Meets Kalulu

The next day Kalulu was walking through the forest when he met Monkey.

Monkey was sitting in a tree eating wild fruits.

“Good morning, Kalulu!” Monkey shouted.

“Good morning, my friend.”

Monkey climbed down.

“You look very happy today.”

Kalulu smiled.

“I know where there is plenty of food.”

Monkey’s eyes opened.

“Food?”

“Yes.”

“What kind?”

“The sweetest sweet potatoes you have ever tasted.”

Monkey jumped excitedly.

“Take me there!”

Kalulu rubbed his chin.

He remembered seeing something strange in the farmer’s garden.

He suspected a trap.

But he wanted to know for sure.

“Alright Monkey,” Kalulu said.

“Follow me.”

The Beautiful Sweet Potato

When they reached the garden, Monkey saw the sweet potato under the basket.

His mouth watered.

“Kalulu!”

“Yes?”

“Look!”

“I see it.”

“It is huge!”

“Yes.”

“Why are you not taking it?”

Kalulu stepped back.

“My friend, since you saw it first, you should have it.”

Monkey smiled.

“You are very kind today.”

Kalulu nodded.

“Very kind.”

Monkey rushed forward.

He grabbed the sweet potato.

Suddenly...

WHOOSH!

The basket fell.

Monkey was trapped.

“Kalulu!”

Monkey shouted.

“Help me!”

Kalulu looked at the basket.

Then he looked around.

“The farmer is coming.”

“What should I do?”

Kalulu smiled.

“Maybe explain that you were only checking if the trap worked.”

“Kalulu!”

But Kalulu was already running away.

The Farmer Arrives

Soon Ba Simukonda arrived.

He saw the basket shaking.

“At last!”

He lifted it.

But instead of Kalulu…

He saw Monkey.

The farmer was surprised.

“Monkey?”

Monkey looked embarrassed.

“I can explain.”

Ba Simukonda folded his arms.

“I was expecting someone with long ears.”

Monkey pointed toward the forest.

“Kalulu tricked me.”

The farmer laughed.

“I should have known.”

He released Monkey.

“Go home. But remember something.”

“What?”

“Do not follow others without thinking.”

Monkey nodded.

“I have learned.”

Kalulu Learns Too

Later, Monkey found Kalulu.

“I thought you were my friend.”

Kalulu became quiet.

For once, he did not laugh.

Monkey continued:

“A clever person should use wisdom to help friends, not put them in danger.”

Kalulu looked down.

Monkey was right.

“I am sorry,” Kalulu said.

From that day, Kalulu still remained clever…

but he slowly learned that cleverness without kindness could hurt others.

🌱 Lesson from the Story

Being clever is good, but wisdom must always go together with kindness.

📚 New Words

  • Trap – something used to catch someone or something
  • Harvest – collecting crops after they grow
  • Suspect – to think something might be wrong
  • Responsibility – doing what is right
