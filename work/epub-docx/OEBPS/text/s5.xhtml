🐘🐇 CHAPTER FIVE

Kalulu and the Elephant’s Challenge

In the great forest where Kalulu lived, every animal had something special.

Bird could fly higher than anyone else.

Cheetah could run faster than anyone else.

Lion had the loudest roar.

Monkey could climb the tallest trees.

And Elephant…

Elephant was the strongest.

Everyone respected Elephant.

When a tree fell across the path, the animals called Elephant.

When a heavy rock blocked the river, they called Elephant.

When something big needed to be carried, everyone shouted:

“Call Elephant!”

And Elephant always came.

He was helpful.

He was kind.

But sometimes Elephant had one problem.

He became too proud of his strength.

Elephant Begins to Boast

One morning, all the animals gathered near the watering hole.

It was a beautiful day.

The birds were singing.

The butterflies flew from flower to flower.

The animals drank water and talked happily.

Suddenly Elephant lifted a huge log with his trunk.

The animals cheered.

“Well done, Elephant!”

“You are very strong!”

“No animal can do that!”

Elephant smiled proudly.

“Yes,” he said.

“I am indeed the strongest animal in this forest.”

The animals became quiet.

Elephant continued:

“With my strength, I can do anything.”

Old Tortoise slowly raised his head.

“Anything?”

“Yes,” said Elephant.

Tortoise smiled.

“Strength is important, my friend. But it is not everything.”

Elephant laughed.

“What can be more important than strength?”

Tortoise replied:

“Wisdom.”

The animals nodded.

But Elephant shook his head.

“Wisdom cannot push down a tree.”

The animals laughed.

“Wisdom cannot carry heavy stones.”

Some animals agreed.

Then Elephant said something that caught everyone’s attention.

“Even clever Kalulu cannot defeat my strength.”

Everyone turned.

Because sitting quietly under a tree…

eating a wild fruit…

was Kalulu.

Kalulu Hears the Challenge

Kalulu slowly looked up.

“Did I hear my name?”

Monkey laughed.

“Elephant says his strength is greater than your wisdom.”

Kalulu stood up.

He walked slowly toward Elephant.

He looked up.

Elephant looked down.

Very far down.

Because Elephant was huge.

And Kalulu was tiny.

The animals started laughing.

“How can these two compete?”

“It is impossible!”

“Elephant could lift Kalulu with one breath!”

Kalulu smiled.

“My friend Elephant, you are very strong.”

Elephant nodded.

“That is true.”

“You can push trees.”

“Yes.”

“You can carry logs.”

“Yes.”

“You can move rocks.”

“Yes.”

Kalulu rubbed his chin.

“But can you defeat me?”

Elephant laughed so loudly the trees shook.

“You?”

“Yes.”

“A small hare?”

“Yes.”

Elephant laughed again.

“Kalulu, I do not even need all my strength.”

Kalulu smiled.

That small Kalulu smile.

The animals knew that smile.

Something was coming.

The Competition

“What challenge do you suggest?” asked Elephant.

Kalulu looked around.

Then he saw a long, strong rope used by the villagers.

“Tug-of-war.”

The animals shouted excitedly.

“A tug-of-war!”

“Elephant against Kalulu!”

“This we must see!”

Monkey climbed higher into the tree.

Bird flew closer.

Even Lion came to watch.

Elephant smiled.

“This will be the easiest competition ever.”

Kalulu picked up the rope.

“We start tomorrow morning.”

“Why tomorrow?” asked Elephant.

Kalulu smiled.

“Great competitions need preparation.”

Elephant laughed.

“You prepare. I will rest.”

And Elephant walked away.

Kalulu’s Secret Plan

That evening, Kalulu carried the rope deep into the forest.

He walked until he reached the biggest tree.

It was an enormous old baobab tree.

Its roots went deep into the earth.

Its trunk was wider than ten animals standing together.

Kalulu tied one end of the rope tightly around the tree.

Then he covered the rope with leaves.

He smiled.

“Tomorrow will be interesting.”

The Great Tug-of-War

The next morning, the forest was full of excitement.

Animals came from everywhere.

“This will be quick,” said Hyena.

“Poor Kalulu,” said Zebra.

“Maybe we should help him after Elephant pulls him away,” said Monkey.

Then Elephant arrived.

Boom.

Boom.

Boom.

Each step shook the ground.

Kalulu arrived shortly after.

Hop.

Hop.

Hop.

The difference was funny.

A giant elephant.

A tiny hare.

Elephant picked up his side of the rope.

“Are you ready, Kalulu?”

Kalulu ran into the bushes.

“I am ready!”

But Elephant could not see that Kalulu was not holding the rope.

The rope was tied to the baobab tree.

“PULL!” shouted Lion.

Elephant pulled.

Nothing happened.

He pulled harder.

Still nothing.

Elephant became surprised.

“Hmmm.”

He wrapped the rope around his trunk.

He pulled again.

HARDER.

The ground moved.

Dust rose.

Birds flew away.

But the rope did not move.

Elephant Uses All His Strength

Elephant became serious.

“No small hare can be this strong.”

He pulled with everything he had.

His feet pushed deep into the ground.

His muscles became tired.

Sweat rolled down his face.

The animals watched in shock.

“Is Kalulu really that strong?” whispered Monkey.

Even Lion was confused.

Finally Elephant fell down.

“I give up!”

The forest became silent.

Then Kalulu walked out from behind the trees.

“Are you finished, my friend?”

Elephant stared.

“How?”

Kalulu showed everyone the rope tied around the baobab tree.

The animals started laughing.

Elephant stood up.

“You tricked me!”

Kalulu nodded.

“Yes.”

Elephant frowned.

“That is not fair.”

Kalulu walked closer.

“My friend, yesterday you said strength was everything.”

Elephant became quiet.

“But today a tree defeated your strength because I used my mind.”

Elephant Understands

Old Tortoise walked forward.

“Elephant, your strength is a gift.”

Elephant listened.

“But gifts become greater when combined with wisdom.”

Elephant looked at Kalulu.

For the first time, he understood.

“I was proud.”

Kalulu smiled.

“And I sometimes play too many tricks.”

The animals laughed.

Elephant lifted Kalulu gently with his trunk.

“From today, we work together.”

Kalulu smiled.

“With your strength…”

“And your wisdom,” Elephant added.

“We can solve any problem.”

And from that day, whenever there was a difficult task in the forest, animals no longer asked:

“Who is strongest?”

They asked:

“What is the wisest way?”

🌱 Lesson from the Story

Strength is useful, but wisdom helps us use strength correctly.

📚 New Words

  • Strength – having power or ability
  • Boast – talking too proudly about yourself
  • Challenge – a test or competition
  • Wisdom – making good choices

🤔 Think About It

  1. Was Elephant wrong to be proud?
  2. Did Kalulu prove his point?
  3. Is intelligence more important than strength?
  4. How can strong people use their abilities to help others?
