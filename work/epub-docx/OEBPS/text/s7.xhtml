🌍 CHAPTER SEVEN:

Chimuka and the Big Bully

This section will move into the classic Zambian school-reader style stories about children, school life, responsibility, honesty, friendship, and courage. 📚✨

CHILDREN OF THE VILLAGE

The elders always said:

"Animals can teach us wisdom, but children show us who we can become."

After the stories of Kalulu, Grandmother would allow the fire to become smaller.

The younger children would move closer.

They loved hearing about talking animals.

They laughed when Hyena was tricked.

They cheered when Kalulu escaped.

But Grandmother wanted them to understand something important.

“Children,” she said, “stories are not only about animals in the forest.”

Little Ngambo looked surprised.

“They are not?”

Grandmother smiled.

“No. Some of the greatest stories are about ordinary boys and girls.”

“Children like us?”

“Yes,” answered Grandmother.

“Children who go to school.”

“Children who make mistakes.”

“Children who learn courage.”

“Children who discover that doing the right thing is not always easy.”

The children became quiet.

The fire cracked softly.

Grandmother looked at them.

“Tonight I will tell you about a boy named Chimuka.”

“Was he strong?” asked one child.

“No.”

“Was he rich?”

“No.”

“Was he famous?”

Grandmother smiled.

“Not at first.”

“Then why do people remember him?”

Grandmother picked up another piece of firewood.

“Because Chimuka had something more powerful than strength.”

“What?”

“A good heart.”

And she began.

🧒 Chimuka and the Big Bully

Every morning in the village of Mulembe, the sound of children walking to school filled the air.

Some walked barefoot along the dusty paths.

Some carried their books in small bags.

Others carried them carefully in their hands.

They laughed.

They sang.

They chased each other between the trees.

School was not just a place for learning.

It was where friendships were made.

It was where children played football using balls made from plastic bags.

It was where they learned lessons they would remember forever.

At the end of the village path stood Mulembe Primary School.

The classrooms were simple.

The windows were always open to allow the fresh morning air inside.

Outside stood a large mango tree.

During break time, children gathered there.

They ate their food.

They told stories.

They played games.

Everyone loved the mango tree.

Everyone except when Kabwe was there.

The Boy Everyone Feared

Kabwe was the biggest boy in the class.

He was taller than everyone.

He could run fast.

He was strong.

Very strong.

But Kabwe did not use his strength well.

When younger children played football, Kabwe took the ball.

When children sat under the mango tree, Kabwe pushed them away.

When someone had nice food, Kabwe demanded some.

Nobody wanted to argue with Kabwe.

“He is too strong,” they said.

“He will beat us.”

So they stayed away.

But staying away only made Kabwe worse.

One Monday morning, a young boy named Chimuka arrived at school.

Chimuka was different.

He was not tall.

He was not the strongest.

He was quiet.

But everyone liked him.

Why?

Because Chimuka respected people.

When someone dropped books, Chimuka helped pick them up.

When younger pupils struggled with homework, Chimuka explained.

When someone had no lunch, Chimuka shared.

His mother always told him:

“My son, a kind heart will take you further than strong hands.”

Chimuka never forgot those words.

Trouble at Break Time

One afternoon, the school bell rang.

KENG! KENG! KENG!

Break time!

The children rushed outside.

Chimuka sat under the mango tree with his friends.

His mother had packed him roasted groundnuts and a small piece of cassava.

Just as he opened his lunch...

Everyone became silent.

Kabwe was coming.

The younger children quickly moved away.

Kabwe smiled.

He enjoyed seeing people afraid.

He walked straight to Chimuka.

“What are you eating?”

Chimuka looked up.

“Groundnuts.”

Kabwe stretched out his hand.

“Give them to me.”

The other children watched.

Nobody spoke.

They expected Chimuka to hand over everything.

But Chimuka did something different.

He smiled.

“Good afternoon, Kabwe.”

Kabwe looked confused.

People usually ran away from him.

They did not greet him.

“I said give me your food,” Kabwe repeated.

Chimuka looked at the groundnuts.

Then he looked at Kabwe.

“I can share with you.”

The children were shocked.

Share?

With Kabwe?

“But I will not give them because you frightened me.”

Kabwe frowned.

“What?”

“You do not need to scare people for them to share with you.”

The Unexpected Answer

For the first time, Kabwe did not know what to say.

He expected crying.

He expected fear.

He expected anger.

But Chimuka showed kindness.

Slowly, Chimuka divided the groundnuts into two piles.

“Here.”

Kabwe looked at them.

“Why are you giving me some?”

“Because maybe you were hungry.”

Kabwe became quiet.

Nobody had asked him that before.

The truth was…

Kabwe often came to school without food.

His family had problems at home.

But instead of telling his friends, he became angry.

He pushed people away before they could reject him.

The Teacher’s Lesson

Their teacher, Mrs Banda, had been watching from the classroom.

She called both boys.

“Kabwe.”

“Yes, Madam.”

“You are strong.”

Kabwe looked down.

“But strength is like fire.”

The boys listened.

“Fire can cook food and keep people warm.”

She paused.

“But the same fire can destroy a house.”

Kabwe looked at her.

“Your strength can protect people or hurt them.”

Kabwe felt ashamed.

“I am sorry, Madam.”

Mrs Banda smiled.

“Do not only say sorry.”

She pointed outside.

“Show it.”

A New Beginning

The next day, something strange happened.

A small boy dropped all his books.

Everyone expected Kabwe to laugh.

But Kabwe walked over.

The little boy became afraid.

Then Kabwe bent down.

And helped him pick them up.

Everyone stared.

At break time, Kabwe sat under the mango tree.

Nobody ran away.

Chimuka smiled.

“Do you want some groundnuts?”

Kabwe laughed.

“Only if you also take some of mine.”

From that day, the biggest boy in school became the biggest helper.

And whenever children talked about bravery, they remembered Chimuka.

Not because he fought.

Not because he shouted.

But because he showed kindness when everyone expected fear.

🌱 Lesson from the Story

True courage is not about fighting others. Sometimes the greatest courage is showing kindness.

📚 New Words

  • Bully – someone who hurts or frightens others
  • Courage – doing what is right even when it is difficult
  • Respect – treating others with kindness
  • Change – becoming better than before
