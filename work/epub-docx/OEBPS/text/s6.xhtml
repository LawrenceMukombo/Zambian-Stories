🐆🐇 CHAPTER SIX

Kalulu and the Leopard

The forest was usually a peaceful place.

In the mornings, birds filled the trees with beautiful songs.

The monkeys jumped from branch to branch.

The antelopes grazed quietly in the open fields.

And near the small hills, Kalulu enjoyed sitting outside his home watching the world around him.

But every forest has danger.

And in this forest, many animals feared one name.

Leopard.

Leopard was one of the finest hunters in the whole land.

He was fast.

He was silent.

He was powerful.

When Leopard walked through the forest, even dry leaves seemed afraid to make noise under his feet.

His yellow eyes could see movement from far away.

His sharp ears could hear the smallest sound.

And his strong legs could climb the tallest trees.

Many animals respected Leopard.

But Leopard had a problem.

He was proud.

Very proud.

Leopard’s Complaint

One afternoon, Leopard was resting on a tree branch.

Below him, Hyena walked by.

Hyena looked unhappy.

“Why do you look as if you swallowed a bitter fruit?” asked Leopard.

Hyena sighed.

“It is Kalulu.”

Leopard opened one eye.

“Kalulu?”

“Yes.”

“The small hare?”

“Yes.”

Leopard laughed.

“What problem can a small hare cause?”

Hyena sat down.

“You do not understand.”

“He tricked Lion.”

Leopard stopped smiling.

“He tricked Lion?”

“Yes.”

“He tricked Elephant.”

Leopard sat up.

“Elephant?”

“Yes.”

“And he tricked the crocodiles.”

Leopard jumped down from the tree.

“That is impossible.”

Hyena shook his head.

“You have never dealt with Kalulu.”

Leopard smiled proudly.

“Maybe Lion was careless.”

“Maybe Elephant was foolish.”

“Maybe the crocodiles were too proud.”

Hyena looked at him.

“And you?”

Leopard lifted his head.

“I am different.”

Leopard Makes a Promise

Later that evening, the animals gathered near the river.

Leopard stood on a rock.

“Listen everyone!”

The animals turned.

“I have heard many stories about Kalulu.”

Monkey smiled.

“Good stories?”

Leopard ignored him.

“You all allowed a tiny hare to embarrass you.”

Elephant frowned.

“Be careful, Leopard.”

But Leopard continued.

“He cannot trick me.”

The animals whispered.

“He sounds like Elephant before the tug-of-war.”

Elephant pretended not to hear.

Leopard raised his voice.

“Before the next full moon, I will catch Kalulu.”

The animals became silent.

Far away behind a bush…

two long ears were listening.

Kalulu.

Kalulu Prepares

Kalulu walked home slowly.

“This Leopard thinks catching me will be easy.”

He sat outside his house.

The moon was shining.

The insects were singing.

But Kalulu was thinking.

“Leopard is different,” he said.

“He is faster than Hyena.”

“He is smarter than Crocodile.”

“He is more patient than Elephant.”

Kalulu knew he had to be careful.

Because sometimes even clever animals meet challenges.

The Chase Begins

The next morning, Kalulu went looking for food.

He found some wild berries near the river.

As he reached for one…

Snap!

A branch broke behind him.

Kalulu froze.

Someone was there.

He looked around.

Nothing.

He listened carefully.

Silence.

Then a voice came from above.

“Good morning, Kalulu.”

Slowly, Kalulu looked up.

Leopard was lying on a tree branch.

Kalulu smiled nervously.

“Ah, Leopard! My good friend!”

Leopard laughed.

“Friend?”

“Yes,” said Kalulu.

“I was just thinking about visiting you.”

Leopard climbed down.

“Really?”

“Of course.”

“Why?”

Kalulu paused.

His mind started racing.

“Because…”

Leopard moved closer.

“Yes?”

“Because I wanted to congratulate you.”

Leopard stopped.

“Congratulate me?”

“Yes.”

“For what?”

Kalulu smiled.

“For becoming the greatest leopard.”

Leopard liked the sound of that.

“The greatest?”

“Yes,” said Kalulu.

“But there is only one problem.”

Leopard frowned.

“What problem?”

Kalulu pointed toward the river.

“There is another leopard who says he is greater than you.”

The Other Leopard

Leopard became angry.

“Another leopard?”

“Yes.”

“In my forest?”

“Yes.”

“Impossible!”

Kalulu shook his head.

“I saw him myself.”

Leopard growled.

“Take me there.”

Kalulu started walking.

Leopard followed.

Soon they reached a high riverbank.

Below them, the water was calm and clear.

Kalulu pointed.

“There.”

Leopard looked.

Deep in the water…

he saw another leopard staring back.

The leopard had yellow eyes.

The leopard had spots.

The leopard looked angry.

Leopard growled.

The leopard in the water growled back.

“He is challenging me!” shouted Leopard.

Kalulu stepped back.

“He looks very strong.”

Leopard became even angrier.

“No leopard is stronger than me.”

Leopard Learns the Truth

Without thinking…

Leopard jumped.

SPLASH!

He landed in the river.

He looked around.

No leopard.

Only water.

Only fish.

Only his own reflection.

Slowly, Leopard understood.

He climbed back onto land.

Wet.

Cold.

Embarrassed.

Kalulu was sitting nearby.

“You tricked me.”

Kalulu nodded.

“Yes.”

Leopard shook himself dry.

“I should be angry.”

“Are you?”

Leopard thought.

Then he smiled.

“No.”

Kalulu was surprised.

“No?”

Leopard sat down.

“I laughed when others were tricked.”

“I thought I was better.”

“I became proud.”

Kalulu nodded.

“Pride makes us stop thinking.”

Leopard looked at the river.

“The leopard I wanted to fight…”

“Yes?”

“Was myself.”

A Different Leopard

From that day, Leopard changed.

He was still fast.

He was still strong.

He was still a great hunter.

But he became humble.

Whenever younger animals praised him, he said:

“Remember, even the fastest legs must listen to a wise mind.”

And Kalulu learned something too.

Not every victory should embarrass someone.

Sometimes wisdom should teach.

Not hurt.

Many years later, animals still told the story:

The day Leopard fought himself in the river.

And the day a small hare taught a great hunter humility.

🌱 Lesson from the Story

Pride can make even intelligent people make mistakes. Humility helps us continue learning.

📚 New Words

  • Reflection – an image seen in water or a mirror
  • Humble – not thinking you are better than others
  • Embarrassed – feeling uncomfortable after making a mistake
  • Patient – able to wait calmly

  4. How can we remain humble when we are good at something?
