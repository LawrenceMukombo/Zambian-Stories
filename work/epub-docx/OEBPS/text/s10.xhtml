🐄 CHAPTER TEN

The Lost Calf

A Story About Responsibility

The rainy season had blessed the village of Mukonde.

Everywhere you looked, there was life.

The hills that had once been brown and dry were now covered with fresh green grass.

The maize fields danced gently whenever the wind passed.

The streams that children crossed on their way to school were once again flowing with clear water.

Birds sang in the trees.

Butterflies moved from flower to flower.

The farmers were happy.

And the animals were even happier.

Because after months of searching for dry grass, the cattle now had plenty to eat.

At the edge of Mukonde village lived a young girl named Mutinta.

Mutinta was ten years old.

She lived with her mother, father, grandmother, and younger brother.

She was a cheerful child.

She loved singing.

She loved playing games.

She loved listening to grandmother’s stories at night.

But sometimes Mutinta had one problem.

She became distracted easily.

When she was doing one thing…

something exciting could quickly make her forget.

Her grandmother often reminded her:

“Mutinta, a person who carries responsibility must carry attention with it.”

Mutinta would always smile.

“Yes, Grandmother.”

But she did not fully understand what those words meant.

Not yet.

The Special Calf

One morning, Mutinta’s father called her.

“Mutinta!”

“Yes, Father!”

She ran outside.

Her father was standing near the cattle shelter.

Beside him stood a beautiful young calf.

The calf was brown with a white mark on its forehead.

Its eyes were gentle.

Its legs were still thin and playful.

Mutinta loved this calf.

Its name was Bana.

“Today I need your help,” Father said.

Mutinta smiled proudly.

“My help?”

“Yes.”

Father pointed to Bana.

“I need you to take Bana to the grazing field.”

Mutinta became excited.

Usually her older cousins looked after the cattle.

This was the first time Father trusted her.

“You mean I can go alone?”

Father nodded.

“You are growing older. You must learn responsibility.”

Mutinta stood tall.

“I will take good care of Bana.”

Father smiled.

“I believe you.”

Then his face became serious.

“But remember…”

Mutinta already knew.

“Watch carefully.”

Father nodded.

“A calf is like a small child. It needs someone responsible.”

A Beautiful Morning

Mutinta walked happily towards the grazing area.

Bana followed behind.

Along the way she sang:

“Little calf, little calf, walking with me, Eating fresh grass under the tree.”

Bana jumped playfully.

Mutinta laughed.

“You are also enjoying today!”

When they reached the field, Bana immediately started eating.

Mutinta sat under a tree.

She felt proud.

“I am looking after cattle,” she said to herself.

“I am no longer a small child.”

For the first hour, everything went well.

Whenever Bana moved too far, Mutinta called:

“Bana!”

The calf came back.

But then…

something happened.

Friends Arrive

From a nearby path came voices.

“Mutinta!”

She turned.

Her friends were coming.

There was Chipo.

There was Lombe.

There was Mwila.

They were carrying a handmade ball.

“We are going to play,” said Chipo.

“Come with us!”

Mutinta looked at Bana.

“I cannot. I am watching the calf.”

Lombe laughed.

“Just play one game.”

“One game only.”

Mutinta looked again.

Bana was quietly eating grass.

The calf looked safe.

“One game,” Mutinta said.

“Then I will come back.”

But one game became two games.

Two games became three.

The children laughed.

They ran.

They shouted.

They forgot about time.

And slowly…

very slowly…

Bana walked away.

Where is Bana?

After some time, Mutinta stopped.

She suddenly remembered.

“Bana!”

She looked towards the tree.

No calf.

She looked near the grass.

No calf.

Her heart started beating faster.

“Bana!”

Nothing.

Her friends stopped playing.

“What happened?” asked Chipo.

“The calf is gone.”

Everyone became quiet.

They searched behind bushes.

Nothing.

They searched near trees.

Nothing.

They searched around the field.

Still nothing.

The sun started moving lower.

Mutinta became afraid.

“What will Father say?”

Chipo tried to comfort her.

“Maybe Bana went home.”

Mutinta shook her head.

“What if something happened?”

“What if Bana is hurt?”

“What if wild animals find her?”

Tears filled her eyes.

The Old Man by the Path

As Mutinta walked along the path searching, she met an old man carrying firewood.

“Child, why are you crying?”

Mutinta wiped her face.

“I lost my father’s calf.”

The old man put down his firewood.

“How did it happen?”

Mutinta became quiet.

She wanted to say:

“I don’t know.”

She wanted to blame someone.

But she remembered Grandmother’s words.

Responsibility.

She looked down.

“I stopped watching because I wanted to play.”

The old man nodded.

“You have already taken the first step.”

Mutinta looked confused.

“What step?”

“Admitting the truth.”

The old man smiled.

“When we make mistakes, hiding them makes them heavier.”

He pointed to the ground.

“Now, stop crying and start thinking.”

Mutinta listened.

“When looking for something lost, use your ears as well as your eyes.”

“My ears?”

“Yes.”

“Be still.”

Listening Carefully

Mutinta stood quietly.

The wind moved through the trees.

Birds sang.

Leaves moved.

Then…

very softly…

“Moooo.”

Mutinta opened her eyes wide.

“Bana!”

Again.

“Moooo.”

The sound came from near the stream.

Mutinta ran.

Behind some tall grass stood Bana.

The calf’s rope had become stuck around a small tree.

“Bana!”

Mutinta hugged the calf.

“I found you!”

Returning Home

When Mutinta reached home, Father was waiting.

She looked down.

“I am sorry.”

Father listened quietly.

“I played with my friends and stopped watching Bana.”

“I made a mistake.”

Father looked at her.

“Were you afraid to tell me?”

“Yes.”

“But you still told the truth.”

Mutinta nodded.

Father smiled.

“Today you lost a calf…”

Mutinta looked sad.

“But you found responsibility.”

She looked up.

“Responsibility?”

“Yes.”

“Everyone makes mistakes. Growing up means learning from them.”

That night, Grandmother told a story around the fire.

But before she started, she looked at Mutinta.

“Today someone learned that responsibility is not just being given a job.”

She smiled.

“It is remembering the trust someone has placed in you.”

Mutinta smiled.

This time, she understood.

🌱 Lesson from the Story

Being responsible means doing what you promised, even when something more exciting appears. Everyone makes mistakes, but honest people admit mistakes and learn from them.

📚 New Words

  • Responsibility – taking care of something trusted to you
  • Distracted – losing attention because of something else
  • Admit – accepting the truth
  • Trust – believing someone will do the right thing
