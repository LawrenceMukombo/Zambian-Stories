🐇 CHAPTER THREE

Kalulu and the Crocodiles

Many seasons passed, but stories about Kalulu continued spreading across the forest.

Wherever animals gathered, someone always had a Kalulu story to tell.

At the river, birds talked about him.

In the trees, monkeys laughed about him.

Even deep in the grasslands, the antelopes whispered about the small hare who could defeat bigger animals using only his mind.

But not everyone admired Kalulu.

Some animals were tired of being tricked.

One group especially wanted to teach him a lesson.

The crocodiles.

The Great River

At the edge of the forest flowed a wide river.

It was not a small stream that children could jump across.

No.

This was a mighty river.

During the rainy season, the water rose high and moved quickly.

The waves crashed against the riverbanks.

Large trees grew beside it, their branches hanging over the water.

Birds built their nests there.

Fishermen came to catch fish.

Animals came to drink.

But everyone knew one important rule:

Never go too close to the water.

Because the river belonged to the crocodiles.

The crocodiles were feared by many animals.

They had powerful tails.

They had strong jaws.

They had sharp teeth.

When they floated quietly in the water, they looked like ordinary logs.

But they were not logs.

They were hunters.

And their leader was an old crocodile named Mamba.

Mamba was bigger than all the others.

His skin was rough from many years in the river.

His teeth were long.

His eyes were sharp.

And there was one animal he disliked more than anyone.

Kalulu.

The Problem Across the River

One morning, Kalulu woke up very hungry.

He searched around his home.

No food.

He checked behind the trees.

Nothing.

He checked near the bushes.

Nothing.

“My stomach is making more noise than Lion’s roar,” Kalulu said.

He decided to walk around and search for food.

After walking for a long time, he reached the river.

Then he saw something.

Across the river was a beautiful garden.

His eyes opened wide.

There were sweet potatoes.

There were pumpkins.

There were fresh vegetables.

There were carrots.

Kalulu rubbed his stomach.

“That,” he said, smiling, “is where my breakfast is waiting.”

But then he looked at the river.

His smile disappeared.

“How will I cross?”

He could not swim across.

The river was too wide.

He could not jump across.

It was too far.

He could not build a bridge.

It would take too long.

Kalulu sat under a tree.

Whenever Kalulu sat quietly, everyone knew something was happening.

He was thinking.

The Crocodiles Appear

Suddenly, two eyes appeared above the water.

Then another pair.

Then another.

Soon, many crocodiles were watching him.

The biggest one moved closer.

It was Mamba.

“Well, well, well,” said Mamba.

“If it is not Kalulu the clever hare.”

Kalulu smiled.

“Good morning, my dear friend Mamba.”

“Friend?” laughed Mamba.

“You call me your friend after all the tricks you have played?”

Kalulu looked innocent.

“Tricks? Me?”

The crocodiles laughed.

“Yes, you!” shouted Mamba.

“You tricked Lion.”

“You tricked Hyena.”

“You tricked Elephant.”

“But today, you cannot trick us.”

Kalulu looked at the river.

Then he looked at the crocodiles.

Then he smiled.

A small smile.

A Kalulu smile.

Kalulu’s Announcement

“Mamba,” said Kalulu seriously, “I am actually happy to see you.”

The crocodile was surprised.

“You are?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Kalulu stood proudly.

“I have been sent by the King of all animals.”

The crocodiles looked at each other.

“The King?” they whispered.

Mamba moved closer.

“What does the King want?”

Kalulu cleared his throat.

“The King wants to know which animals are the greatest in the kingdom.”

The crocodiles smiled.

“That is easy,” said Mamba.

“We are.”

Kalulu nodded.

“I thought so too.”

The crocodiles became proud.

“But there is a problem,” continued Kalulu.

“What problem?”

“The King needs to know exactly how many crocodiles live in this river.”

Mamba lifted his head.

“How many?”

“Yes,” said Kalulu.

“I must count every crocodile and take the number back to the King.”

The crocodiles became excited.

“At last!” said one crocodile.

“The King has recognized our greatness!”

Counting the Crocodiles

Mamba gave an order.

“All crocodiles, line up!”

Immediately crocodiles came from everywhere.

Young crocodiles.

Old crocodiles.

Small crocodiles.

Large crocodiles.

They formed a long line across the river.

From one side…

all the way…

to the other side.

Kalulu looked carefully.

The crocodiles had created a perfect bridge.

Exactly as he planned.

“Are you ready?” asked Mamba.

“Yes,” said Kalulu.

He jumped onto the first crocodile.

“One!”

He jumped onto the second.

“Two!”

Then the third.

“Three!”

The crocodiles smiled proudly.

Kalulu continued jumping.

“Four!”

“Five!”

“Six!”

Soon he was halfway across.

The crocodiles stayed very still.

Nobody wanted to spoil the counting.

Kalulu continued.

“Ten!”

“Eleven!”

“Twelve!”

Finally…

Hop!

Kalulu landed safely on the other side of the river.

The Truth Comes Out

“Kalulu!” shouted Mamba.

“Yes?” replied Kalulu.

“How many crocodiles are there?”

Kalulu smiled.

“Enough.”

The crocodiles became confused.

“Enough?”

“Yes.”

“Enough for what?”

Kalulu laughed.

“Enough to help me cross the river!”

For a moment, nobody spoke.

Then Mamba understood.

“KALULU!”

The river shook with angry crocodile voices.

“You tricked us!”

Kalulu picked up a sweet potato from the garden.

“My friends, you said nobody could trick you.”

The crocodiles looked embarrassed.

Kalulu continued:

“You were so busy thinking about your greatness that you forgot to ask questions.”

Mamba became quiet.

He knew Kalulu was right.

A Lesson by the River

Later that evening, when Kalulu returned home, the animals heard what had happened.

Monkey laughed until he nearly fell from the tree.

“Kalulu crossed the river using crocodiles!”

Even Elephant smiled.

But the oldest tortoise spoke wisely.

“Do not only laugh,” he said.

“There is a lesson here.”

The animals listened.

“Strength is useful. But pride can make even the strongest person foolish.”

Everyone nodded.

And across the river, even Mamba remembered those words.

From that day forward, whenever someone praised him too much, he first stopped and thought:

“Am I being wise, or am I becoming proud?”

And somewhere in the forest…

Kalulu smiled.

🌱 Lesson from the Story

Being strong or powerful is good, but wisdom and humility are even more important.

📚 New Words

  • Pride – thinking too highly of yourself
  • Recognize – to notice or appreciate something
  • Careful – thinking before acting
  • Challenge – something difficult to overcome

  3. What happens when people become too proud?
  4. How can strength and wisdom work together?
