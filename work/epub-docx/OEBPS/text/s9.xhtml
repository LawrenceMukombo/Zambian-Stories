🌊 CHAPTER NINE

Journey to Tanzania – Part Two

A New World by the Ocean

The bus continued moving through the night.

Inside, almost everyone was asleep.

Some passengers rested their heads against the windows.

Others covered themselves with jackets because the night air had become cold.

The driver kept his eyes on the long road ahead as the headlights cut through the darkness.

But there was one passenger who struggled to sleep.

Ngambo.

He tried closing his eyes.

He tried resting his head.

But every few minutes he opened his eyes and looked outside.

Mwansa noticed.

“Why are you still awake?”

Ngambo smiled.

“I am thinking.”

“About what?”

“Tanzania.”

Mwansa laughed quietly.

“Tanzania will still be there tomorrow. Sleep.”

But Ngambo could not stop wondering.

What would Dar es Salaam look like?

Would he understand the language?

Would the ocean really be as big as people said?

There were so many questions.

Finally, while looking through the window at the stars, he slowly fell asleep.

Arrival in Dar es Salaam

Early the next morning, Ngambo woke up because the bus had stopped.

He opened his eyes.

Something was different.

Very different.

The air felt different.

It smelled different.

He sat up.

“Mwansa.”

Mwansa was still sleeping.

“Mwansa!”

“What?”

“We are here!”

Mwansa quickly looked outside.

The quiet roads and forests were gone.

Now they saw:

Tall buildings.

Busy streets.

Cars everywhere.

People walking quickly.

Traders selling goods.

Motorcycles moving between vehicles.

They had arrived in Dar es Salaam.

The bus station was full of activity.

People were greeting relatives.

Taxi drivers were calling passengers.

Families were carrying bags.

Ngambo stepped down from the bus and looked around.

“So many people,” he whispered.

Mwansa smiled.

“Welcome to the city.”

Suddenly they heard a familiar voice.

“Ngambo!”

They turned.

A tall man was waving.

It was Uncle Joseph.

Meeting Uncle Joseph

The boys ran towards him.

“My nephews!”

He hugged them happily.

“You have grown!”

Ngambo laughed.

“Everyone says that.”

“Because it is true!”

Uncle Joseph helped them carry their bags.

“How was the journey?”

“Long,” said Mwansa.

“Very long.”

“But exciting,” added Ngambo.

Uncle Joseph smiled.

“That is travelling. Sometimes tiring, but always teaching you something.”

As they drove through the city, Ngambo looked everywhere.

He saw shops.

Markets.

Schools.

People selling fruit.

Children walking in uniforms.

“It is different,” Ngambo said.

“Yes,” Uncle Joseph replied.

“But also the same.”

“What do you mean?”

“People everywhere want similar things.”

He pointed outside.

“Families.”

“Friends.”

“Education.”

“A better future.”

Ngambo thought carefully about those words.

Then he opened his notebook and wrote them down.

Learning New Words

When they arrived at Uncle Joseph’s house, his neighbour came outside.

“Karibuni!”

Ngambo looked confused.

Uncle Joseph smiled.

“She said welcome.”

“Oh.”

The neighbour smiled.

“Habari?”

Ngambo whispered:

“What does that mean?”

“She is asking how you are.”

“What should I say?”

“Say nzuri.”

Ngambo smiled nervously.

“Nzuri.”

Everyone laughed kindly.

That evening, Uncle Joseph taught them some simple Swahili.

Jambo – Hello

Asante – Thank you

Rafiki – Friend

Karibu – Welcome

Ngambo enjoyed learning.

“I thought travelling was only seeing places,” he said.

Uncle Joseph shook his head.

“No.”

“Travelling is also meeting people.”

The First Time Seeing the Ocean

The next morning, Uncle Joseph woke them early.

“Today is a special day.”

“Where are we going?” asked Mwansa.

Uncle Joseph smiled.

“You will see.”

They drove through the city.

Soon, Ngambo noticed something.

The air changed.

It became cooler.

There was a fresh smell.

Then he heard something.

Waves.

The car stopped.

“Come.”

The boys walked forward.

Then they saw it.

The Indian Ocean.

Ngambo stood still.

He had seen pictures in books.

But pictures were small.

This was different.

The water stretched further than his eyes could see.

The waves moved again and again.

Birds flew above.

Ships appeared far away like tiny toys.

For a long time, Ngambo said nothing.

Finally Mwansa laughed.

“You are very quiet.”

Ngambo smiled.

“I did not know water could be this much.”

Everyone laughed.

New Friends

At the beach, the boys met some Tanzanian children playing football.

At first, Ngambo was shy.

He did not know them.

They spoke differently.

But one boy kicked the ball toward him.

“You play?”

Ngambo smiled.

“Yes.”

Soon they were all running and laughing.

Nobody cared about accents.

Nobody cared about countries.

Football became their language.

After the game, the boy introduced himself.

“My name is Hassan.”

“I am Ngambo.”

“You are from Zambia?”

“Yes.”

“I have never been there.”

“And I had never been here.”

They laughed.

The Market Visit

Another day, Uncle Joseph took them to the market.

It was full of colours.

Red tomatoes.

Yellow bananas.

Green vegetables.

Beautiful fabrics.

Spices filled the air.

“This market smells different,” said Mwansa.

Uncle Joseph laughed.

“Those are spices.”

They tasted new foods.

Chapati.

Fresh fish.

Coconut.

Mandazi.

Ngambo wrote everything in his notebook.

A Lesson from Uncle Joseph

Their last evening arrived too quickly.

The boys sat outside with Uncle Joseph.

“Tomorrow you return home,” he said.

Ngambo looked sad.

“I wish we could stay longer.”

“That means you enjoyed your journey.”

“Yes.”

Uncle Joseph smiled.

“Then remember what you learned.”

Ngambo opened his notebook.

He read:

“People may speak different languages.”

“They may eat different foods.”

“They may live in different countries.”

“But kindness is understood everywhere.”

Uncle Joseph nodded.

“That is the greatest lesson from travelling.”

Returning Home

The next morning, they boarded the bus back to Zambia.

This time something was different.

When Ngambo first travelled, he carried only clothes in his bag.

Now he carried something more important.

Memories.

Stories.

Knowledge.

Experience.

When he arrived home, his little sister ran to him.

“What did you bring me?”

Ngambo smiled and handed her a small gift.

Then she asked:

“What did you see?”

Ngambo looked at his notebook.

Then he sat down.

Everyone gathered around.

Just like children gathering for stories under the baobab tree.

And Ngambo began:

“Let me tell you about my journey to Tanzania…”

🌱 Lesson from the Story

The world becomes smaller when people learn about each other. Respect and friendship can cross any border.

📚 New Words

  • Ocean – a very large body of salt water
  • Language – words people use to communicate
  • Tradition – beliefs and practices passed through generations
  • Experience – knowledge gained by doing something
